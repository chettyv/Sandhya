If this is the first year the puja is yours to run, start with this reassurance: **a sincere, simple Lakshmi puja done with attention is a real puja.** The elaborate versions you may have seen — a priest, a long Sanskrit liturgy, dozens of items — are one way of doing it, not the entry requirement. Households have always done shorter forms, and the goddess of the home is honoured in the language of the home.

## What you need (the honest minimum)

- A small clean space — a shelf, a corner of a table, facing whichever direction your family uses (east or north-east is common; families differ).
- An image or murti of Lakshmi — a framed picture is fine. Many families include Ganesha alongside, honoured first as the remover of obstacles.
- A diya (lamp) with ghee or oil, and something to light it with.
- Water in a small vessel; a few flowers if you have them; something sweet to offer — fruit, a little sugar, anything made with care.
- Optional and traditional where available: incense, kumkum or haldi, rice grains, coins (Lakshmi puja often honours wealth in its dignified sense — sufficiency, generosity, prosperity that circulates).

Nothing on this list is a test. Missing items are replaced by attention.

## A gentle sequence

1. **Clean the space**, then bathe or wash hands and face — arriving clean is itself part of the practice.
2. **Light the diya.** Take one breath before moving on.
3. **Honour Ganesha first** if your family does — a simple namaskar and his name.
4. **Invite Lakshmi.** Fold your hands and welcome her as you would an honoured guest — in Sanskrit if you know your family's verses, or in your own language. Speaking plainly is old, respectable practice: _"Goddess, be welcome in this home."_
5. **Offer what you brought** — water, flowers, the sweet. Each offering is placed or gestured toward the image with attention.
6. **Say the names or prayer you know.** If your family recites a particular stotra or the names of Lakshmi, use theirs. If you know nothing yet, sit for a minute of quiet gratitude for what sustains your household — that is not a placeholder; it is the heart of the thing.
7. **Aarti** if you have the means — circling the lit lamp before the image while singing or in silence.
8. **Close** with a namaskar, and share the offered sweet (prasad) with everyone at home.

Ten minutes done with care beats an hour done anxiously from a script you don't trust.

## When is it done?

Most famously on **Diwali night** — in much of India, Lakshmi puja is _the_ central act of Diwali evening — and also on Fridays, on Sharad Purnima, and during Navratri in many homes. Regional practice differs widely: Bengali households celebrate Lakshmi Puja (Lokkhi Pujo) on the full moon after Durga Puja rather than at Diwali, and in South India the emphases and dates differ again. Your family's calendar is the right one to follow.

## The question underneath

Most people searching for this are really asking: _am I allowed to do this without knowing what I'm doing?_ Yes. Every person who runs a household puja learned by doing it uncertainly the first time. Ask an elder what your family includes if you can; where you can't ask, begin simply and let the practice grow. The tradition has always been carried exactly this way.
