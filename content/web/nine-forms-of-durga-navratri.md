Across the nine nights of Navratri, many Hindus honour the goddess in nine forms — the **Navadurga**. Each night is associated with one form, from Shailaputri on the first night to Siddhidatri on the ninth. Here is the widely followed sequence, with the meaning of each name and what the form is generally understood to represent.

## The nine forms, night by night

1. **Shailaputri** — "daughter of the mountain." The goddess as daughter of Himavat, lord of the Himalayas. A beginning rooted in stability and the natural world.
2. **Brahmacharini** — the goddess as ascetic, representing discipline, austerity, and devoted practice.
3. **Chandraghanta** — "she who bears the crescent moon like a bell." Courage combined with serenity; the protector who is also gentle.
4. **Kushmanda** — often understood as the source of the cosmic egg, the one whose smile brings forth the world. Creative energy and warmth.
5. **Skandamata** — mother of Skanda (Kartikeya, Murugan). The goddess as mother, holding her child; love that protects.
6. **Katyayani** — the warrior form, born of the sage Katyayana's devotion. Fierce resolve against what is wrong.
7. **Kalaratri** — the dark, fierce night form; terrifying to what is harmful, protective to the devotee. Fear faced and dissolved.
8. **Mahagauri** — the radiant, purified form. Calm after the storm; forgiveness and renewal.
9. **Siddhidatri** — "giver of siddhis," the bestower of attainment and completion. The journey's fruit.

## Is this the only way Navratri is observed?

No — and it matters to say so. The Navadurga sequence is widely followed, especially in North India, but it is one pattern among several:

- In **Bengal and much of the east**, these nights culminate in **Durga Puja**, centred on Durga's victory over Mahishasura — the emphasis is on the great goddess herself, her arrival with her children, and community celebration.
- In **Gujarat**, the nights belong to **garba and dandiya raas**, danced around the goddess or her lamp — worship expressed as dance.
- In **South India**, many homes keep a **golu** (a stepped display of figures), and the final days honour **Saraswati** and **Lakshmi**; in Karnataka the festival is tied to the royal Dasara of Mysore.
- Some traditions organise the nine nights as three sets of three — honouring **Durga, Lakshmi, and Saraswati** — rather than nine distinct forms.

If your family's Navratri doesn't match the list above, that is not a corrupted version of the festival. It is one of its living regional forms.

## Where does the Navadurga list come from?

The names are traditionally connected with the goddess tradition of texts such as the Devi Mahatmya (part of the Markandeya Purana) and later devotional literature, and the nightly sequence as popularly observed owes as much to living custom as to any single text. Treat the list as devotional practice with deep roots — not as a verse-by-verse scriptural prescription.
