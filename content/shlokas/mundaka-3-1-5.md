## Shloka

**Devanagari:** सत्येन लभ्यस्तपसा ह्येष आत्मा / सम्यग्ज्ञानेन ब्रह्मचर्येण नित्यम् । / अन्तःशरीरे ज्योतिर्मयो हि शुभ्रो / यं पश्यन्ति यतयः क्षीणदोषाः ॥3.1.5॥

**Meaning:** By truth this Self can be won — by tapas, by right knowledge, by constant brahmacharya. Within the body he dwells, made of light, radiant; the strivers whose faults have worn thin see him.

## Word by word

- **satyena** — by truth, by truthfulness
- **labhyaḥ** — is attainable, can be won
- **tapasā** — by tapas, by disciplined effort and austerity
- **hi eṣaḥ ātmā** — indeed this Self
- **samyak-jñānena** — by right, complete knowledge
- **brahmacaryeṇa** — by brahmacharya, continence and student-like discipline
- **nityam** — constantly
- **antaḥśarīre** — within the body
- **jyotirmayaḥ** — made of light
- **hi śubhraḥ** — radiant, pure white
- **yam paśyanti** — whom they see
- **yatayaḥ** — the strivers, the ascetics
- **kṣīṇadoṣāḥ** — whose faults have worn thin, dwindled

## Meaning

The Self is not far away, and yet it has a price list — not money, but a way of living: truthfulness, sustained effort, clear understanding, and self-restraint held steadily rather than heroically for a weekend. The verse's most encouraging word may be kshinadoshah, "whose faults have worn thin." It does not say faultless. Faults are not smashed in one blow; they are thinned by daily practice, the way a rope wears through. And the seeing happens antah-sharire, inside this very body — the search does not require leaving anywhere. Practically every Hindu tradition receives this verse warmly, differing mainly on whether these disciplines directly reveal the Self or prepare the heart for grace to do so.

## Meaning (hi)

आत्मा दूर नहीं है, फिर भी उसका एक मूल्य है — पैसा नहीं, जीने का ढंग: सत्य, सतत प्रयास, स्पष्ट समझ, और संयम — जो किसी एक सप्ताहांत की वीरता नहीं, रोज़ की स्थिरता माँगता है। श्लोक का सबसे उत्साह देने वाला शब्द शायद "क्षीणदोषाः" है — "जिनके दोष क्षीण हो गए हैं।" वह "निर्दोष" नहीं कहता। दोष एक प्रहार में नहीं टूटते; वे रोज़ के अभ्यास से घिसते हैं, जैसे रस्सी धीरे-धीरे घिस जाती है। और दर्शन "अन्तःशरीरे" होता है — इसी शरीर के भीतर; खोज के लिए कहीं जाना नहीं पड़ता। लगभग सभी हिंदू परंपराएँ इस श्लोक को अपनाती हैं; अंतर बस इतना है कि ये साधन आत्मा को सीधे प्रकट करते हैं या हृदय को कृपा के लिए तैयार करते हैं।
