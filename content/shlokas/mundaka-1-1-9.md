## Shloka

**Devanagari:** यः सर्वज्ञः सर्वविद्यस्य ज्ञानमयं तापः । / तस्मादेतद्ब्रह्म नाम रूपमन्नं च जायाते ॥1.1.9॥

<!-- review note: Two probable transcription artifacts, left unaltered per policy (the IAST mirrors them): standard editions read "तपः" (yasya jñānamayaṃ tapaḥ) where the source has "तापः", and "जायते" where the source has "जायाते". "सर्वविद्यस्य" itself is fine — it is the regular sandhi of सर्ववित् + यस्य. -->

**IAST:** yaḥ sarvajñaḥ sarvavidyasya jñānamayaṃ tāpaḥ । / tasmādetadbrahma nāma rūpamannaṃ ca jāyāte ॥1.1.9॥
**Say it:** yah sar-va-GYAH sar-va-vid YAS-ya gyaa-na-ma-YUM TAA-pah / tas-MAAD ay-tad BRAH-ma NAA-ma ROO-pum an-NUM cha jaa-YAA-tay
**Meaning:** He who is all-knowing, wise to each thing, whose tapas is made of knowledge itself — from him is born this Brahma: name, form, and food.
**Meaning (hi):** जो सर्वज्ञ है, जो एक-एक बात का भी ज्ञाता है, जिसका तप ज्ञान से ही बना है — उसी से यह ब्रह्म जन्म लेता है: नाम, रूप और अन्न।

## Word by word

- **yaḥ** — he who
- **sarvajñaḥ** — all-knowing, knowing all as a whole
- **sarvavit** — all-wise, knowing each thing in its detail
- **yasya** — whose
- **jñānamayam** — made of knowledge
- **tapaḥ** — tapas, creative heat (printed here as tāpaḥ)
- **tasmāt** — from him
- **etat brahma** — this Brahma — read by the commentators as the first-born creative whole
- **nāma** — name
- **rūpam** — form
- **annam ca** — and food
- **jāyate** — is born (printed here as jāyāte)

## Meaning

The previous verse said creation begins with tapas; this one says whose tapas, and of what it is made. Not fasting, not fire, not austerity of the limbs — the all-knower's tapas is knowledge itself: reality is thought into being, not laboured into being. The classical gloss distinguishes the two epithets — sarvajña, knowing everything as a whole; sarvavit, knowing each thing in particular — omniscience in both the wide-angle and the close-up. And "this brahma" that is born is read in the commentaries as the manifest creative power, the world of name, form, and food, not a second absolute — a commentarial clarification, worth labelling as such. Everything nameable descends, the verse insists, from an act of knowing.

## Meaning (hi)

पिछले श्लोक ने कहा था कि सृष्टि तप से शुरू होती है; यह बताता है — किसका तप, और वह बना किससे है। न उपवास, न अग्नि, न देह की तपस्या — सर्वज्ञ का तप स्वयं ज्ञान है: सत्ता श्रम से नहीं, जानने से जन्मती है। शास्त्रीय टीका दोनों विशेषणों में भेद करती है — 'सर्वज्ञ' यानी समग्र का ज्ञाता, 'सर्ववित्' यानी एक-एक ब्योरे का ज्ञाता — सर्वज्ञता चौड़े और बारीक, दोनों लेंसों में। और जो "यह ब्रह्म" जन्म लेता है, उसे भाष्य नाम-रूप-अन्न का प्रकट सृजन-जगत पढ़ते हैं, कोई दूसरा परम तत्त्व नहीं — यह भाष्य की व्याख्या है, और उसी रूप में कही जानी चाहिए। श्लोक का आग्रह इतना है: जो कुछ भी नाम रखता है, वह किसी जानने की क्रिया से उतरा है।

## Reflection

Is there anything you know so thoroughly that the knowing itself has begun to shape what you create?
