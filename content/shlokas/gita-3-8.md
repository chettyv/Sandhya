## Shloka

**Devanagari:** नियतं कुरु कर्म त्वं कर्म ज्यायो ह्यकर्मणः । / शरीरयात्रापि च ते न प्रसिद्ध्येदकर्मणः ॥3.8॥
**IAST:** niyataṃ kuru karma tvaṃ karma jyāyo hyakarmaṇaḥ । / śarīrayātrāpi ca te na prasiddhyedakarmaṇaḥ ॥3.8॥
**Say it:** ni-ya-TUM KOO-roo KAR-ma tvum KAR-ma JYAA-yo hya-KAR-ma-nah / sha-REE-ra-YAA-traa-pi cha tay na pra-SID-dhyay-da-KAR-ma-nah
**Meaning:** Do the work that is yours to do, for action is better than inaction; without action, even keeping your body going would be impossible.

## Word by word

- **niyatam** — prescribed, ordained, allotted
- **kuru** — do, perform
- **karma** — action, work
- **tvam** — you
- **jyāyaḥ** — better, superior
- **hi** — for, indeed
- **akarmaṇaḥ** — than inaction
- **śarīra-yātrā** — the upkeep of the body, bodily maintenance
- **api ca** — even, and also
- **te** — your
- **na prasiddhyet** — would not be accomplished
- **akarmaṇaḥ** — through inaction

## Meaning

Krishna makes the practical case against opting out: doing nothing is not actually available to you. Even staying alive — eating, moving, breathing — is action, so the choice is never between action and inaction, only between doing your allotted work and doing something lesser. For daily practice the verse is bracingly simple: identify the work that is genuinely yours today and do it, rather than drifting through substitute activity. Traditions differ on what "prescribed action" ultimately means — Advaita commentators emphasise ordained duties as purification that readies the mind for knowledge, while Vishishtadvaita and Dvaita read the same duties as ongoing worship offered to the Lord — but all agree the duty itself is not optional.

## Reflection

What is the one piece of work that is genuinely yours today, and what substitute activity are you tempted to do instead?
