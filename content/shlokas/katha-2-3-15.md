## Shloka

**IAST:** yathā sarve prabhidyante hṛdayasyeha granthayaḥ । / atha martyo'mṛto bhavatyetāvaddhyanuśāsanam ॥2.3.15॥

**Meaning:** When all the knots of the heart are cut through, here in this life, then a mortal becomes immortal. This much is the whole teaching.

## Word by word

- **yathā** — when, as soon as (most printed editions read yadā, "when")
- **sarve** — all
- **prabhidyante** — are cut through, split open
- **hṛdayasya** — of the heart
- **iha** — here, in this life
- **granthayaḥ** — the knots
- **atha** — then
- **martyaḥ amṛtaḥ bhavati** — the mortal becomes immortal
- **etāvat hi** — this much indeed
- **anuśāsanam** — is the teaching

## Meaning

The previous verse spoke of desires released; this one hardens the image into granthi — knots, the places where awareness has been lashed to what it is not: "I am this body, this role, this history." Cutting them is not self-mutilation; it is the undoing of a false splice. And then the Upanishad does something rare: it stops. Etāvad dhy anuśāsanam — this much is the teaching. No further secret is being withheld, no higher initiation is waiting behind a curtain. A text about the ultimate ends by declaring its own completeness, which is itself a final lesson: what remains now is not more instruction but the doing.

## Meaning (hi)

पिछले श्लोक में कामनाएँ छूटती थीं; यह श्लोक उस चित्र को "ग्रन्थि" में कस देता है — गाँठें, वे जगहें जहाँ चेतना उससे बाँध दी गई है जो वह नहीं है: "मैं यह शरीर हूँ, यह पद, यह इतिहास।" इन्हें काटना आत्म-हानि नहीं; एक झूठी जोड़ का खुलना है। और फिर उपनिषद कुछ दुर्लभ करता है: रुक जाता है। "एतावद्ध्यनुशासनम्" — बस इतनी ही शिक्षा है। कोई और रहस्य रोका नहीं गया, कोई ऊँची दीक्षा पर्दे के पीछे प्रतीक्षा में नहीं। परम विषय पर लिखा ग्रंथ अपनी पूर्णता घोषित करके समाप्त होता है — और यह स्वयं एक अंतिम पाठ है: अब शेष उपदेश नहीं, करना है।
