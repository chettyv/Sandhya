## Shloka

**Devanagari:** मयि सर्वाणि कर्माणि संन्यस्याध्यात्मचेतसा । / निराशीर्निर्ममो भूत्वा युध्यस्व विगतज्वरः ॥3.30॥
**IAST:** mayi sarvāṇi karmāṇi saṃnyasyādhyātmacetasā । / nirāśīrnirmamo bhūtvā yudhyasva vigatajvaraḥ ॥3.30॥
**Say it:** MA-yi sar-VAA-ni kar-MAA-ni sun-nyas-YAA-dhyaat-ma-CHAY-ta-saa / ni-RAA-sheer NEER-ma-mo BHOOT-vaa YOO-dhyas-va vi-ga-ta-JVA-rah
**Meaning:** Surrendering all actions to me, with mind resting in the Self, free of craving and possessiveness, fight — with the fever gone.

## Word by word

- **mayi** — in me, to me (Krishna)
- **sarvāṇi** — all
- **karmāṇi** — actions
- **saṃnyasya** — surrendering, offering up
- **adhyātma-cetasā** — with mind fixed on the Self, with inward awareness
- **nirāśīḥ** — free from craving, without expectation
- **nirmamaḥ** — without "mine"-ness, without possessiveness
- **bhūtvā** — having become
- **yudhyasva** — fight
- **vigata-jvaraḥ** — with the fever gone, free of anguish

## Meaning

Krishna's instruction to Arjuna is startlingly complete: hand every action over to me, drop the craving and the "mine," and then — fight. Surrender here does not mean retreat from difficulty; it means entering the difficulty without the fever, the anxious inner burning that comes from carrying outcomes alone. For daily practice, whatever your battle is today — a diagnosis, a deadline, a conflict — the verse offers a sequence: offer it up first, release the grasping, then act wholeheartedly. Traditions read the surrender differently: Advaita commentators take it as casting actions on the Lord with the discernment that the Self is not the agent, while Vishishtadvaita and Dvaita hear a literal call to loving surrender of every act to Krishna as its true master.

## Reflection

What battle are you facing with a fever of anxiety — and what would it mean to offer it up before you fight it?
