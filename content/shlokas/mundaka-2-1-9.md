## Shloka

**Devanagari:** अतः समुद्रा गिरयश्च सर्वेऽस्मात् / स्यन्दन्ते सिन्धवः सर्वरूपाः । / अतश्च सर्वा ओषधयो रसश्च / येनैष भूतैस्तिष्ठते ह्यन्तरात्मा ॥2.1.9॥

**Meaning:** From him are all the seas and the mountains; from him stream the rivers of every form; from him come all the plants, and the sap by which the inner self stands firm amid the elements that body it.

## Word by word

- **ataḥ** — from him
- **samudrāḥ** — the seas
- **girayaḥ ca** — and the mountains
- **sarve** — all
- **asmāt** — from this one
- **syandante** — flow, stream
- **sindhavaḥ** — the rivers
- **sarva-rūpāḥ** — of every form
- **ataḥ ca** — and from him
- **sarvāḥ oṣadhayaḥ** — all plants
- **rasaḥ ca** — and the sap, the essence in food
- **yena** — by which
- **eṣaḥ antar-ātmā** — this inner self
- **bhūtaiḥ tiṣṭhate** — stands firm amid the elements
- **hi** — indeed

## Meaning

The survey comes home to the landscape: oceans, mountains, rivers of every form, plants — and finally rasa, sap, the nourishing essence in food. That last item makes the verse intimate. The commentators note that it is by this essence that the inner self stands amid the elements: your subtle life is held in place, day after day, by juice drawn from plants, which draw from rain, which the sequence has already traced to the Purusha. Geography and lunch belong to one system of generosity. To eat attentively — tasting the long line from source to sap to strength — is this verse's most literal practice.

## Meaning (hi)

अब यह सर्वेक्षण धरती के भूगोल पर लौटता है: समुद्र, पर्वत, "हर रूप की" नदियाँ, वनस्पतियाँ — और अंत में रस, अन्न का पोषक सत्व। यही आख़िरी शब्द श्लोक को आत्मीय बना देता है। भाष्यकार बताते हैं कि इसी रस के सहारे अन्तरात्मा "भूतों के बीच" टिका है: तुम्हारा सूक्ष्म जीवन रोज़ उस सत्व से थमा है जो पौधों से आता है, पौधे वर्षा से, और वर्षा — यह क्रम बता चुका है — पुरुष से। भूगोल और भोजन एक ही उदारता के तंत्र के अंग हैं। ध्यान से खाना — स्रोत से रस और रस से बल तक की लंबी कड़ी को चखते हुए — इस श्लोक का सबसे सीधा अभ्यास है।

## Reflection

At your next meal, can you taste the river, the rain, and what stands behind them both?
