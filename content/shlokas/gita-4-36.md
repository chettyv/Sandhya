## Shloka

**Devanagari:** अपि चेदसि पापेभ्यः सर्वेभ्यः पापकृत्तमः । / सर्वं ज्ञानप्लवेनैव वृजिनं सन्तरिष्यसि ॥4.36॥
**IAST:** api cedasi pāpebhyaḥ sarvebhyaḥ pāpakṛttamaḥ । / sarvaṃ jñānaplavenaiva vṛjinaṃ santariṣyasi ॥4.36॥
**Say it:** A-pi CHED a-si paa-PAY-bhyah sar-VAY-bhyah paa-pa-KRIT-ta-mah / SAR-vam gyaa-na-pla-VAY-nai-va vri-JI-nam san-ta-RISH-ya-si
**Meaning:** Even if you were the most sinful of all sinners, you would cross over all wrongdoing by the boat of knowledge alone.

## Word by word

- **api** — even
- **cet** — if
- **asi** — you are
- **pāpebhyaḥ sarvebhyaḥ** — than all sinners
- **pāpa-kṛttamaḥ** — the worst doer of wrong
- **sarvam** — all
- **jñāna-plavena** — by the boat of knowledge
- **eva** — alone, indeed
- **vṛjinam** — wrongdoing, crookedness
- **santariṣyasi** — you will cross over

## Meaning

One of the Gita's most hopeful verses. Krishna takes the extreme case on purpose — even the worst wrongdoer of all — and says the boat of knowledge still carries that person across. The image matters: a flood of past mistakes is not drained away drop by drop; it is crossed, in a vessel, once you climb in. No one is disqualified by their history. For daily practice, this cuts against the paralysis of guilt: the response to having done wrong is not endless self-punishment but honest understanding — seeing clearly what you did, why, and who you actually are beneath the act. Regret that leads to knowledge becomes a boat; regret that only circles becomes the flood.

## Reflection

What past mistake are you still treading water in, instead of building the understanding that would carry you across?
