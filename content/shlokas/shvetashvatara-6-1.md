## Shloka

**IAST:** svabhāvameke kavayo vadanti kālaṃ tathānye parimuhyamānāḥ । / devasyaiṣa mahimā tu loke yenedaṃ bhrāmyate brahmacakram ॥6.1॥

**Meaning:** Some seers say it is inherent nature; others, just as bewildered, say time. But it is the greatness of God in the world by which this wheel of Brahman is turned.

## Word by word

- **svabhāvam** — inherent nature (as the world's cause)
- **eke kavayaḥ** — some sages
- **vadanti** — declare
- **kālam** — time
- **tathā anye** — likewise others
- **parimuhyamānāḥ** — utterly bewildered
- **devasya** — of God
- **eṣaḥ mahimā tu** — but this is the greatness
- **loke** — in the world
- **yena** — by which
- **idam brahma-cakram** — this wheel of Brahman
- **bhrāmyate** — is made to revolve

## Meaning

The crowning chapter opens by returning to the question the Upanishad asked in its very first verses: what turns the world? Some thinkers say svabhāva — things simply behave as things do; others say time grinds everything forward. The verse calls both parties learned and both bewildered, because each mistakes an instrument for the hand that holds it. What turns this brahma-cakra, the great wheel of Brahman, is the mahimā — the majesty — of God. The Shvetashvatara is the most openly theistic of the principal Upanishads: Shaiva tradition hears this deva as Rudra-Shiva, Vishishtadvaita as Narayana, Advaita as the one Brahman in personal aspect — and the chapter itself simply says: God.

## Meaning (hi)

अंतिम अध्याय उसी प्रश्न पर लौटकर खुलता है जिससे उपनिषद शुरू हुआ था: जगत को घुमाता कौन है? कुछ विचारक कहते हैं स्वभाव — चीज़ें बस अपनी प्रकृति से चलती हैं; दूसरे कहते हैं काल सबको पीसता चलता है। श्लोक दोनों पक्षों को विद्वान भी कहता है और भ्रमित भी, क्योंकि दोनों औज़ार को ही हाथ समझ बैठे हैं। इस ब्रह्मचक्र को घुमाती है देव की महिमा। श्वेताश्वतर प्रधान उपनिषदों में सबसे स्पष्ट ईश्वरवादी है: शैव परंपरा इस देव में रुद्र-शिव को सुनती है, विशिष्टाद्वैत नारायण को, अद्वैत सगुण रूप में उसी एक ब्रह्म को — और अध्याय स्वयं बस इतना कहता है: ईश्वर।

## Reflection

When you explain how your life is going, what do you credit — habit, luck, time — and what would change if you named the wheel-turner instead?
