## Shloka

**Devanagari:** विद्याविनयसंपन्ने ब्राह्मणे गवि हस्तिनि । / शुनि चैव श्वपाके च पण्डिताः समदर्शिनः ॥5.18॥
**IAST:** vidyāvinayasaṃpanne brāhmaṇe gavi hastini । / śuni caiva śvapāke ca paṇḍitāḥ samadarśinaḥ ॥5.18॥
**Say it:** vid-YAA-vi-na-ya-sum-PUN-nay BRAAH-ma-nay GU-vi HUS-ti-ni / SHU-ni CHAI-va shva-PAA-kay cha PUN-di-taah sa-ma-DAR-shi-nah
**Meaning:** The wise look with an equal eye upon a brahmin endowed with learning and humility, a cow, an elephant, a dog, and an outcaste.

## Word by word

- **vidyā-vinaya-saṃpanne** — endowed with learning and humility
- **brāhmaṇe** — in a brahmin
- **gavi** — in a cow
- **hastini** — in an elephant
- **śuni** — in a dog
- **ca eva** — and even
- **śvapāke** — in an outcaste (literally "dog-cooker," the lowest social rank of the time)
- **ca** — and
- **paṇḍitāḥ** — the wise, the learned
- **sama-darśinaḥ** — seeing the same, with equal vision

## Meaning

The verse deliberately runs from the most honoured figure of its society to the most despised, adding two animals in between, and says the wise see the same in all of them. Bodies, ranks, and species differ wildly; what dwells within does not differ in worth. This is sama-darshana — equal vision — and the Gita treats it as a mark of real knowledge, not mere politeness. For daily practice, catch yourself mid-ranking: the person you defer to and the person you overlook. Traditions genuinely diverge on what exactly is equal: Advaita sees one identical Self in all, Vishishtadvaita sees selves alike in essence though embodied differently, and Dvaita sees distinct souls each equally indwelt by the Lord — the verse leaves room for each reading.

## Reflection

Who did you look past or quietly rank beneath you today, and what changes if you look again with an equal eye?
