## Shloka

**Devanagari:** त्रिरुन्नतं स्थाप्य समं शरीरं हृदीन्द्रियाणि मनसा सन्निवेश्य । / ब्रह्मोडुपेन प्रतरेत विद्वान्स्रोतांसि सर्वाणि भयानकानि ॥2.8॥

**IAST:** trirunnataṃ sthāpya samaṃ śarīraṃ hṛdīndriyāṇi manasā sanniveśya । / brahmoḍupena pratareta vidvānsrotāṃsi sarvāṇi bhayānakāni ॥2.8॥

**Meaning:** Holding the body steady with its three parts — chest, neck, head — raised in a line, letting the senses and the mind settle into the heart, the wise one crosses on the raft of Brahman all the rivers of fear.

## Word by word

- **triḥ unnatam** — with the three held high (chest, neck, and head, as the classical commentaries take it)
- **sthāpya** — having set, holding steady
- **samam śarīram** — the body even, aligned
- **hṛdi** — in the heart
- **indriyāṇi** — the senses
- **manasā** — together with the mind
- **sanniveśya** — having settled, gathered in
- **brahma-uḍupena** — by the raft of Brahman
- **pratareta** — may cross over
- **vidvān** — the wise one
- **srotāṃsi** — the streams, the currents
- **sarvāṇi bhayānakāni** — all of them filled with fear

## Meaning

Here begins the earliest systematic sketch of yoga practice in the principal Upanishads (2.8–15), and it begins with posture. The text describes rather than instructs: a body steady and aligned, senses and mind gathered into the heart — the same upright ease the Bhagavad Gita later echoes. Note where the verse says this is going: not performance but a ferry-crossing, for the streams are "fear-filled," and the raft is Brahman. Commentators in Shankara's line take the raft as the syllable oṃ; more theistic readers hear God himself carrying the crosser — both agree the practitioner does not cross alone. These verses were always transmitted face to face: read them as a map of the terrain, and let a qualified teacher be the road.

## Meaning (hi)

प्रधान उपनिषदों में योगाभ्यास का सबसे पुराना क्रमबद्ध चित्र यहीं से शुरू होता है (2.8–15), और शुरुआत आसन से होती है। पाठ निर्देश नहीं देता, वर्णन करता है: स्थिर, सधा हुआ शरीर; हृदय में सिमटे हुए मन और इंद्रियाँ — वही सहज सीधापन जिसकी गूँज आगे भगवद्गीता में मिलती है। ध्यान दीजिए कि श्लोक की मंज़िल क्या है: प्रदर्शन नहीं, नदी पार करना — धाराएँ "भय-भरी" हैं, और नौका है ब्रह्म। शंकर-परंपरा के टीकाकार इस नौका को ॐ पढ़ते हैं; भक्ति-प्रधान पाठक स्वयं ईश्वर को, जो पार ले जाता है — दोनों मानते हैं कि साधक अकेला नहीं तैरता। ये श्लोक सदा गुरु-मुख से सीखे जाते रहे हैं: इन्हें भू-दृश्य का नक्शा समझिए, और रास्ता किसी योग्य शिक्षक से पूछिए।

## Reflection
