**Meaning:** Your face — faintly smiling, spotless, like the orb of the full moon, lovely with the lustre of the finest gold, utterly wondrous — and yet, having looked on it, Mahishasura, seized by rage, struck at it at once. (The sentence continues in the next verse.)

## Word by word

- **īṣat-sahāsam** — faintly smiling
- **amalam** — spotless
- **paripūrṇa-candra-bimba-anukāri** — resembling the orb of the full moon
- **kanaka-uttama-kānti-kāntam** — lovely with the lustre of the finest gold
- **ati-adbhutam** — utterly wondrous
- **prahṛtam** — (was) struck at
- **ātta-ruṣā** — by one who had taken up rage
- **tathā api** — and yet
- **vaktram** — (your) face
- **vilokya** — having looked on
- **sahasā** — at once, rashly
- **mahiṣāsureṇa** — by Mahishasura

## Meaning

The sentence begun here finishes in 4.13, so this is a reader's verse rather than a daily one. It is a portrait, placed deliberately in the middle of a hymn of power: the Devi's face, faintly smiling, spotless, moonlike, golden. Then the turn: this was the face Mahishasura looked at, and he struck at it anyway. The hymn's astonishment is not at the violence of the blow but at the blindness behind it. ātta-ruṣā — "having taken up anger" — is telling: rage is picked up like a weapon, and once held it makes a person hit at the very thing that should have stopped the hand.

## Meaning (hi)

यहाँ शुरू हुआ वाक्य 4.13 में पूरा होता है, इसलिए यह पढ़ने का श्लोक है, रोज़ का नहीं। यह एक चित्र है, जानबूझकर शक्ति के स्तोत्र के बीचोबीच रखा हुआ: देवी का मुख — हल्की मुस्कान, निर्मल, चन्द्रमा-सा, सुनहरा। फिर मोड़: यही वह मुख था जिसे महिषासुर ने देखा, और फिर भी उस पर प्रहार किया। स्तोत्र का विस्मय प्रहार की हिंसा पर नहीं, उसके पीछे के अन्धेपन पर है। आत्त-रुषा — "क्रोध को उठा लेने वाला" — बताने वाला पद है: क्रोध हथियार की तरह उठाया जाता है, और थाम लेने पर वह उसी पर वार करवाता है जिसे देखकर हाथ रुक जाना चाहिए था।

## Reflection

What have you lashed out at in anger that, looked at calmly, was actually beautiful?
