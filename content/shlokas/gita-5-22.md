## Shloka

**Devanagari:** ये हि संस्पर्शजा भोगा दुःखयोनय एव ते । / आद्यन्तवन्तः कौन्तेय न तेषु रमते बुधः ॥5.22॥
**IAST:** ye hi saṃsparśajā bhogā duḥkhayonaya eva te । / ādyantavantaḥ kaunteya na teṣu ramate budhaḥ ॥5.22॥
**Say it:** yay hi sum-spar-sha-JAA BHO-gaa DUH-kha-YO-na-ya AY-va tay / aad-YUN-ta-vun-tah kown-TAY-ya na TAY-shoo RA-ma-tay BU-dhah
**Meaning:** The enjoyments born of contact with the senses are truly wombs of sorrow; they have a beginning and an end, O son of Kunti — the wise take no delight in them.

## Word by word

- **ye** — which
- **hi** — indeed
- **saṃsparśa-jāḥ** — born of contact (of the senses with their objects)
- **bhogāḥ** — enjoyments, pleasures
- **duḥkha-yonayaḥ** — wombs of sorrow, sources of suffering
- **eva** — truly
- **te** — they
- **ādi-anta-vantaḥ** — having a beginning and an end
- **kaunteya** — O son of Kunti (Arjuna)
- **na teṣu ramate** — does not delight in them
- **budhaḥ** — the wise one

## Meaning

The verse's diagnosis is structural, not moralising. Any pleasure that comes from contact — sense meeting object — has a start and therefore a stop, and everything with a stop carries its own ending inside it, like a womb carries a birth. The pleasure is not evil; it is simply unreliable, and building your happiness on it means building on something scheduled to leave. The wise are not joyless; they just stop banking on the unstable. For daily practice, watch one habitual pleasure today from start to finish and notice where the enjoyment actually ends. Commentators across Advaita, Vishishtadvaita, and Dvaita read this verse in essentially the same way — a shared warning, not a point of dispute.

## Reflection

Which pleasure do you keep returning to even though you already know exactly how it ends?
