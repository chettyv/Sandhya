## Shloka

**Devanagari:** उद्धरेदात्मनात्मानं नात्मानमवसादयेत् । / आत्मैव ह्यात्मनो बन्धुरात्मैव रिपुरात्मनः ॥6.5॥
**IAST:** uddharedātmanātmānaṃ nātmānamavasādayet । / ātmaiva hyātmano bandhurātmaiva ripurātmanaḥ ॥6.5॥
**Say it:** ood-dha-ray-DAAT-ma-NAAT-maa-num NAAT-maa-na-ma-va-SAA-da-yet / aat-MY-va HYAAT-ma-no BUN-dhoo-raat-MY-va ri-poo-RAAT-ma-naha
**Meaning:** Let a person lift himself up by his own self; let him not drag himself down. For the self alone is the friend of the self, and the self alone is the enemy of the self.

## Meaning

The meditation chapter opens with responsibility: raise yourself by your own self, and do not drag yourself down. No teacher, ritual, or circumstance can do the inner work for you — and the verse names the reason. The same mind that talks you out of practice is the only instrument you have for practice; it is your closest friend and your nearest enemy, depending on how you train it. For daily life this closes two exits at once: waiting to be rescued, and blaming what happened to you. The one relationship you can never outsource is the one with your own mind, so tend it like the friendship it can become.

## Reflection

Where today did you talk yourself down when you could have lifted yourself up?
