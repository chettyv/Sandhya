## Shloka

**Devanagari:** तव स्तन्यं मन्ये धरणिधरकन्ये हृदयतः / पयःपारावारः परिवहति सारस्वतमिव । / दयावत्या दत्तं द्रविडशिशुरास्वाद्य तव यत् / कवीनां प्रौढानामजनि कमनीयः कवयिता ॥75॥

**IAST:** tava stanyaṃ manye dharaṇidharakanye hṛdayataḥ / payaḥpārāvāraḥ parivahati sārasvatamiva । / dayāvatyā dattaṃ draviḍaśiśurāsvādya tava yat / kavīnāṃ prauḍhānāmajani kamanīyaḥ kavayitā ॥75॥
**Say it:** TA-va STAN-yum MAN-yay dha-ra-ni-dha-ra-KAN-yay hri-da-ya-tah / pa-yah-paa-raa-VAA-rah pa-ri-VA-ha-ti saa-ras-va-tam i-va / da-yaa-vat-YAA DAT-tum dra-vi-da-shi-shu-RAAS-vaad-ya TA-va yat / ka-VEE-naam prow-DHAA-naam A-ja-ni ka-ma-NEE-yah ka-va-yi-taa
**Meaning:** O daughter of the earth-bearing mountain, I hold that your breast-milk is an ocean of milk flowing from your very heart as eloquence itself; for the child of the Dravida land, tasting it as the gift of your compassion, arose as the beloved poet among the master-poets.
**Meaning (hi):** हे धरणीधर पर्वत की पुत्री! मैं मानता हूँ कि आपका स्तन्य आपके हृदय से उमड़ता दूध का सागर है — मानो साक्षात् सारस्वत-रस, वाणी का अमृत; क्योंकि आपकी दया से दिया गया वह दूध चखकर द्रविड़-शिशु प्रौढ़ कवियों के बीच मनोहर कवि होकर उभरा।

## Word by word

- **tava stanyam** — your breast-milk
- **manye** — I hold, I believe
- **dharaṇi-dhara-kanye** — O daughter of the earth-bearing mountain
- **hṛdayataḥ** — from the heart
- **payaḥ-pārāvāraḥ** — an ocean of milk
- **parivahati** — streams forth, overflows
- **sārasvatam iva** — as if it were eloquence itself, Sarasvati's essence
- **dayāvatyā dattam** — given by you, the compassionate
- **draviḍa-śiśuḥ** — the child of the Dravida land
- **āsvādya** — having tasted
- **tava yat** — that (milk) of yours
- **kavīnām prauḍhānām** — among mature master-poets
- **ajani** — was born, arose
- **kamanīyaḥ kavayitā** — the charming, beloved poet

## Meaning

Here the poem explains its own existence. All poetry, this verse says, is the Mother's milk: the ocean flowing from her heart is sārasvata — eloquence, the very stuff of speech — and compassion is how it is served. Tradition tells of a Dravida child who, given that milk directly, grew into the beloved of master poets: Shankara hagiographies claim the infant Shankara himself, while Tamil Shaiva tradition tells the nearly identical story of the child-saint Jnanasambandar fed by the Goddess — the verse itself names no one. What it does assert is a theology of talent: whatever flows well in us — speech, art, insight — was nursed into us by a kindness we did not earn.

## Meaning (hi)

यहाँ काव्य अपने ही जन्म का रहस्य खोलता है। यह श्लोक कहता है कि सारी कविता माँ का दूध है: उनके हृदय से बहता क्षीर-सागर स्वयं 'सारस्वत' है — वाणी का तत्त्व — और परोसा जाता है करुणा के हाथों। परम्परा एक द्रविड़-शिशु की कथा कहती है जिसे वह दूध सीधे मिला और जो प्रौढ़ कवियों का प्रिय कवि बना: शंकर-चरित की परम्परा इसे बाल-शंकर मानती है, जबकि तमिळ शैव परम्परा लगभग यही कथा देवी के दूध से पले बाल-सन्त ज्ञानसम्बन्दर की सुनाती है — श्लोक स्वयं कोई नाम नहीं लेता। वह बस प्रतिभा का तत्त्व-दर्शन कहता है: हममें जो भी सहज बहता है — वाणी, कला, सूझ — वह किसी अनर्जित करुणा का पिलाया हुआ है।

## Reflection

Which gift flows through you so naturally that you forget it was once fed to you by someone's kindness?
