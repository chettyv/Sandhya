## Shloka

**Devanagari:** अनपेक्षः शुचिर्दक्ष उदासीनो गतव्यथः । / सर्वारम्भपरित्यागी यो मद्भक्तः स मे प्रियः ॥12.16॥
**IAST:** anapekṣaḥ śucirdakṣa udāsīno gatavyathaḥ । / sarvārambhaparityāgī yo madbhaktaḥ sa me priyaḥ ॥12.16॥
**Say it:** a-na-PAYK-shah SHU-chir DAK-sha u-daa-SEE-no ga-ta-VYA-thah / sar-VAA-ram-bha-pa-ri-TYAA-gee yo mad-BHAK-tah sa may PRI-yah
**Meaning:** One who wants nothing, who is pure, capable, impartial, and untroubled, who has given up all self-interested undertakings — that devotee of mine is dear to me.

## Word by word

- **anapekṣaḥ** — expecting nothing, free of wants
- **śuciḥ** — pure, clean
- **dakṣaḥ** — capable, skilful
- **udāsīnaḥ** — impartial, sitting apart from taking sides
- **gata-vyathaḥ** — untroubled, whose anguish has gone
- **sarva-ārambha-parityāgī** — who has given up all (self-interested) undertakings
- **yaḥ mat-bhaktaḥ** — that devotee of mine
- **saḥ me priyaḥ** — is dear to me

## Meaning

The qualities here are strikingly practical: wanting nothing extra, cleanliness inner and outer, capability in what one does, impartiality, freedom from anguish, and letting go of self-driven schemes. "Giving up all undertakings" cannot mean idleness — the Gita has spent chapters insisting on action — and commentators across traditions vary in nuance here: most take it as dropping projects driven purely by personal gain, or dropping the anxious sense of being the doer, rather than dropping work itself. For a daily practice, the pairing of dakṣa (skilful) with udāsīna (unattached) is the heart of the verse: do your work well, and hold it lightly. Those two together, not either alone, mark the dear devotee.

What expectation could you set down to travel lighter today?
