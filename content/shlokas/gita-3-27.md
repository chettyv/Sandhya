## Shloka

**Devanagari:** प्रकृतेः क्रियमाणानि गुणैः कर्माणि सर्वशः । / अहंकारविमूढात्मा कर्ताहमिति मन्यते ॥3.27॥
**IAST:** prakṛteḥ kriyamāṇāni guṇaiḥ karmāṇi sarvaśaḥ । / ahaṃkāravimūḍhātmā kartāhamiti manyate ॥3.27॥

**Meaning:** All actions are carried out by the gunas of nature; one whose mind is deluded by ego thinks, "I am the doer."

## Word by word

- **prakṛteḥ** — of material nature
- **kriyamāṇāni** — being performed, being carried out
- **guṇaiḥ** — by the gunas, the qualities of nature
- **karmāṇi** — actions
- **sarvaśaḥ** — in every way, entirely
- **ahaṃkāra-vimūḍha-ātmā** — one whose mind is deluded by ego
- **kartā** — the doer
- **aham** — I
- **iti** — thus (marks the quoted thought)
- **manyate** — thinks, believes

## Meaning

Every action you take runs on machinery you did not build — body, temperament, habit, circumstance, all of it the play of nature's gunas. The ego then steps in and signs its name to the finished work: "I did that." Krishna calls this signature the core delusion. For daily practice, the verse loosens the grip of both pride in success and crushing guilt in failure — much of what happens through you is nature moving. The schools genuinely divide here: Advaita holds the Self never acts at all, so all doership belongs to prakriti, while Vishishtadvaita and Dvaita maintain the soul is a real but dependent agent, and the error lies in claiming independent doership apart from nature and the Lord.

## Reflection

Think of something you are proud of or ashamed of from this week — how much of it was truly "you," and how much was nature moving through you?
