## Shloka

**Devanagari:** न हि ज्ञानेन सदृशं पवित्रमिह विद्यते । / तत्स्वयं योगसंसिद्धः कालेनात्मनि विन्दति ॥4.38॥
**IAST:** na hi jñānena sadṛśaṃ pavitramiha vidyate । / tatsvayaṃ yogasaṃsiddhaḥ kālenātmani vindati ॥4.38॥
**Say it:** na hi gyaa-NAY-na sa-DRI-sham pa-VIT-ram i-ha VID-ya-tay / tat SVA-yam yo-ga-sam-SID-dhah kaa-LAY-naat-ma-ni VIN-da-ti
**Meaning:** Nothing in this world purifies like knowledge; one perfected through yoga finds it, in time, within oneself.

## Word by word

- **na** — not
- **hi** — indeed
- **jñānena** — to knowledge
- **sadṛśam** — comparable, equal
- **pavitram** — purifier
- **iha** — here, in this world
- **vidyate** — exists, is found
- **tat** — that (knowledge)
- **svayam** — oneself, on one's own
- **yoga-saṃsiddhaḥ** — one perfected through yoga
- **kālena** — in time, in due course
- **vindati** — finds
- **ātmani** — in oneself

## Meaning

Two claims in one verse. First: of everything that purifies — rivers, rituals, austerities — nothing equals knowledge, because knowledge cleans the root where confusion lives, not just the surface. Second, and easy to miss: this knowledge is found within oneself, in time, by one ripened through practice. It is not handed over finished; the teacher points (as 4.34 says), but the finding happens inside, and it has a season. For daily practice, both halves matter — keep practising even when nothing seems to be happening, and stop expecting insight on demand. Kālena, "in time," is Krishna's own patience clause: steady discipline now, discovery when you are ready.

## Reflection

Where are you demanding instant clarity from a practice that is quietly working on its own schedule?
