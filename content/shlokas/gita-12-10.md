## Shloka

**Devanagari:** अभ्यासेऽप्यसमर्थोऽसि मत्कर्मपरमो भव । / मदर्थमपि कर्माणि कुर्वन्सिद्धिमवाप्स्यसि ॥12.10॥
**IAST:** abhyāse'pyasamartho'si matkarmaparamo bhava । / madarthamapi karmāṇi kurvansiddhimavāpsyasi ॥12.10॥
**Say it:** ab-HYAA-say pya-sa-MAR-tho si mat-kar-ma-pa-ra-MO BHA-va / ma-dar-THAM a-pi kar-MAA-ni KOOR-van SID-dhim a-VAAP-sya-si
**Meaning:** If you cannot even manage this practice, then devote yourself to working for me; even by doing actions for my sake you will reach fulfilment.

## Word by word

- **abhyāse** — in repeated practice
- **api** — even
- **asamarthaḥ** — unable, not capable
- **asi** — you are
- **mat-karma-paramaḥ** — devoted above all to work for me
- **bhava** — become
- **mad-artham** — for my sake
- **api** — even, merely
- **karmāṇi** — actions
- **kurvan** — performing
- **siddhim** — fulfilment, perfection
- **avāpsyasi** — you will attain

## Meaning

The ladder descends another rung. If even structured, repeated practice is beyond you right now, Krishna offers something anyone can do: act for his sake. No meditation posture, no fixed discipline — just take the work already in your hands and dedicate it. Cook, drive, care for someone, do your job, but do it as an offering rather than only for yourself. The verse promises that this alone leads to fulfilment, because the reorientation of motive is itself transformative. For someone starting a daily practice, this is the most forgiving entry point in the Gita: begin where you are, with what you are already doing, and let dedication do the early work that concentration cannot yet do.
