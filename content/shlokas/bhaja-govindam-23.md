**Say it:** KAS-tvum KO-hum KU-ta aa-YAA-tah / kaa may JA-na-nee ko may TAA-tah / I-ti pa-ri-BHAA-va-ya SAR-vam a-SAA-rum / VISH-vum tyakt-VAA SVAP-na-vi-CHAA-rum
**Meaning:** Who are you? Who am I? From where have I come? Who is my mother, who my father? Ponder it all this way, see it as pithless, and let go of the world — it is the stuff of a dream.

## Word by word

- **kaḥ tvam** — who are you
- **kaḥ aham** — who am I
- **kutaḥ āyātaḥ** — from where come
- **kā me jananī** — who is my mother
- **kaḥ me tātaḥ** — who my father
- **iti paribhāvaya** — reflect thus, all the way round
- **sarvam asāram** — everything without essence, pithless
- **viśvam tyaktvā** — letting the world go
- **svapna-vicāram** — (seeing it) as the stuff of a dream

## Meaning

Verse 8 asked these questions about others — whose are you, who is your son. This stanza brings them all the way home: who am I? The instruction paribhāvaya means to think something through on every side until it yields, and what yields here is the assumption that name, lineage, and history are the core of a person rather than the packaging. Asāra, pithless, is a precise image: a reed looks solid until you split it. The dream comparison is Advaita's signature move — a dream is vividly experienced yet makes no lasting claim on the waker. The verse does not ask you to abandon your parents; it asks you to find what in you was never born to them.

## Meaning (hi)

आठवें छन्द ने ये प्रश्न दूसरों के बारे में पूछे थे — तुम किसके हो, कौन तुम्हारा पुत्र। यह छन्द उन्हें पूरा घर तक ले आता है: मैं कौन हूँ? "परिभावय" का अर्थ है किसी बात को हर ओर से तब तक विचारना जब तक वह खुल न जाए — और यहाँ जो खुलता है, वह यह मान्यता है कि नाम, वंश और इतिहास व्यक्ति का मर्म हैं, आवरण नहीं। "असार" — निःसार — बिम्ब सटीक है: सरकंडा ठोस दिखता है, जब तक चीरा न जाए। स्वप्न की उपमा अद्वैत की पहचान है — स्वप्न अनुभव में सजीव होता है, पर जागे हुए पर उसका कोई स्थायी दावा नहीं चलता। छन्द माता-पिता को त्यागने को नहीं कहता; वह उस चीज़ को खोजने को कहता है जो तुममें कभी उनसे जन्मी ही नहीं।

## Reflection

Strip away your name, family, and history for one quiet moment — what remains that still says "I"?
