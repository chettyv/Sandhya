## Shloka

**Devanagari:** ध्यायतो विषयान्पुंसः सङ्गस्तेषूपजायते । / सङ्गात्संजायते कामः कामात्क्रोधोऽभिजायते ॥2.62॥
**IAST:** dhyāyato viṣayānpuṃsaḥ saṅgasteṣūpajāyate । / saṅgātsaṃjāyate kāmaḥ kāmātkrodho'bhijāyate ॥2.62॥
**Say it:** DHYAA-ya-to vi-sha-YAAN POOM-sah SUN-gas tay-SHOO-pa-JAA-ya-tay / sun-GAAT san-JAA-ya-tay KAA-mah kaa-MAAT KRO-dho-bhi-JAA-ya-tay
**Meaning:** When a person keeps dwelling on the objects of the senses, attachment to them arises; from attachment desire is born, and from desire anger arises.

## Word by word

- **dhyāyataḥ** — for one who keeps dwelling on
- **viṣayān** — the objects of the senses
- **puṁsaḥ** — of a person
- **saṅgaḥ** — attachment
- **teṣu** — to them
- **upajāyate** — arises
- **saṅgāt** — from attachment
- **saṁjāyate** — is born
- **kāmaḥ** — desire
- **kāmāt** — from desire
- **krodhaḥ** — anger
- **abhijāyate** — arises

## Meaning

This is the Gita's precise psychology of how self-control fails. It never starts with the dramatic act; it starts with dhyāyataḥ — dwelling on, replaying, browsing. Attention lingers on an object, lingering becomes attachment, attachment sharpens into desire, and desire blocked becomes anger. The verse matters for practice because it locates the point of intervention early: you cannot easily fight a full-grown craving, but you can notice where attention is parked. Watching what the mind rehearses in idle moments is the daily discipline this verse suggests. Commentators across traditions read this chain in essentially the same way.

## Reflection

What do you keep returning to in thought — and where is it leading you?
