## Shloka

**Devanagari:** इति ते ज्ञानमाख्यातं गुह्याद्‌गुह्यतरं मया । / विमृश्यैतदशेषेण यथेच्छसि तथा कुरु ॥18.63॥
**IAST:** iti te jñānamākhyātaṃ guhyādguhyataraṃ mayā । / vimṛśyaitadaśeṣeṇa yathecchasi tathā kuru ॥18.63॥
**Say it:** i-ti tay GYAA-na-MAAKH-yaa-tum gooh-YAAD gooh-ya-ta-rum ma-YAA / vi-mrish-YIGH-ta-da-SHAY-shay-na ya-THAYCH-cha-si ta-THAA koo-roo
**Meaning:** Thus I have declared to you this knowledge, more secret than secrecy itself. Reflect on it fully — and then do as you choose.

## Word by word

- **iti** — thus
- **te** — to you
- **jñānam** — knowledge
- **ākhyātam** — has been declared
- **guhyāt guhyataram** — more secret than the secret
- **mayā** — by me
- **vimṛśya** — having reflected on
- **etat** — this
- **aśeṣeṇa** — fully, without remainder
- **yathā icchasi** — as you wish
- **tathā kuru** — so do

## Meaning

After seventeen chapters of teaching, Krishna does something remarkable: he stops teaching. The entire discourse ends not with a command but with "reflect on it fully, then do as you choose" — yathecchasi tathā kuru. The teacher who could compel instead hands the decision back, making Arjuna's choice, and the reader's, genuinely free. Tradition treasures this as the model of how sacred knowledge is transmitted: offered completely, never forced. The two conditions matter as much as the freedom — reflect (don't react), and reflect fully (aśeṣeṇa, leaving nothing out). For daily practice it is a discipline for decisions: gather the wisdom, think it all the way through, then own your choice.

## Reflection

Which pending decision have you been waiting for someone else to make — and what would choosing it fully, after honest reflection, look like?
