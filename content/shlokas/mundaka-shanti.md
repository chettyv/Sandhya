**Meaning:** Om. May we hear what is auspicious with our ears, O gods; may we see what is auspicious with our eyes, O you worthy of worship. With steady limbs and bodies may we, singing your praise, live out the full span of life the gods have set. May Indra of ancient fame bless us; may the all-knowing Pushan bless us; may Tarkshya of the unbroken wheel bless us; may Brihaspati grant us well-being. Om — peace, peace, peace.

## Word by word

- **oṃ** — the sacred syllable
- **bhadram** — what is auspicious, good
- **karṇebhiḥ** — with our ears
- **śruṇuyāma** — may we hear
- **devāḥ** — O gods
- **paśyema** — may we see
- **akṣabhiḥ** — with our eyes
- **yajatrāḥ** — O you worthy of worship
- **sthiraiḥ aṅgaiḥ** — with steady limbs
- **tuṣṭuvām̐saḥ** — praising, offering praise
- **tanūbhiḥ** — with our bodies
- **vyaśema** — may we live out, attain
- **devahitam yat āyuḥ** — the lifespan the gods have set
- **svasti** — well-being
- **naḥ** — for us
- **indraḥ vṛddhaśravāḥ** — Indra of ancient fame
- **pūṣā viśvavedāḥ** — Pushan, the all-knowing nourisher
- **tārkṣyaḥ ariṣṭanemiḥ** — Tarkshya of the unharmed wheel-rim
- **bṛhaspatiḥ** — Brihaspati, lord of sacred speech
- **dadhātu** — may he grant
- **śāntiḥ** — peace

## Meaning

Before a single teaching is spoken, teacher and student ask for working senses and an unbroken life: ears that take in the good, eyes that see the good, limbs steady enough to serve — because a body in distress makes a poor student. Then four named guardians — Indra, Pushan, Tarkshya, Brihaspati — are each asked for svasti, well-being on the road. This shanti from the Atharvaveda tradition is recited before the Mundaka, Prashna, and Mandukya Upanishads, and it stands complete on its own as a morning prayer. The threefold "shanti" is explained in the commentarial tradition — an explanation the tradition supplies, not the mantra itself — as calming disturbance from three directions: from within ourselves, from other beings, and from forces beyond anyone's control.

## Meaning (hi)

कोई भी शिक्षा शुरू होने से पहले गुरु और शिष्य मिलकर बस इतना माँगते हैं — काम करती इंद्रियाँ और बिना टूटा जीवन। कान शुभ सुनें, आँखें शुभ देखें, अंग स्थिर रहें — क्योंकि दुखता हुआ शरीर पढ़ने नहीं देता। फिर चार रक्षकों — इन्द्र, पूषा, तार्क्ष्य, बृहस्पति — से राह का मंगल माँगा जाता है। अथर्ववेद परंपरा का यह शान्ति-पाठ मुण्डक, प्रश्न और माण्डूक्य उपनिषदों से पहले बोला जाता है, और अपने आप में एक पूर्ण प्रार्थना है। तीन बार 'शान्तिः' कहने की व्याख्या भाष्य-परंपरा देती है — मंत्र स्वयं नहीं: बाधाएँ तीन ओर से आती हैं — अपने भीतर से, दूसरे प्राणियों से, और उन शक्तियों से जो किसी के वश में नहीं।

## Reflection

If your ears and eyes let in only what is worth keeping today, what would you refuse at the gate?
