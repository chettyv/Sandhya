## Shloka

**Devanagari:** लभन्ते ब्रह्मनिर्वाणमृषयः क्षीणकल्मषाः । / छिन्नद्वैधा यतात्मानः सर्वभूतहिते रताः ॥5.25॥
**IAST:** labhante brahmanirvāṇamṛṣayaḥ kṣīṇakalmaṣāḥ । / chinnadvaidhā yatātmānaḥ sarvabhūtahite ratāḥ ॥5.25॥
**Say it:** la-BHUN-tay brah-ma-nir-VAA-num RI-sha-yah KSHEE-na-KUL-ma-shaah / chhin-na-DVAI-dhaa ya-TAAT-maa-nah sar-va-BHOO-ta-hi-tay ra-TAAH
**Meaning:** The seers whose faults are worn away and whose doubts are cut, who are self-restrained and delight in the welfare of all beings, attain the stillness of Brahman.

## Word by word

- **labhante** — attain, obtain
- **brahma-nirvāṇam** — the stillness of Brahman (brahma-nirvana)
- **ṛṣayaḥ** — the seers, the sages
- **kṣīṇa-kalmaṣāḥ** — whose faults are worn away
- **chinna-dvaidhāḥ** — whose doubts, whose dividedness, are cut
- **yata-ātmānaḥ** — self-restrained, with mind reined in
- **sarva-bhūta-hite** — in the welfare of all beings
- **ratāḥ** — delighting, devoted

## Meaning

Notice the last qualification on the list: the sages who reach the highest stillness are the ones who delight in the good of every being. The verse quietly corrects a common picture of spirituality as a private escape — here, inner freedom and care for others arrive together, not in competition. The other marks are practical too: faults worn thin by practice, dividedness cut (no longer living two lives), the self reined in. For daily practice, the most actionable item is the last: do one thing today whose only beneficiary is someone else. Traditions differ over whether brahma-nirvana means merging into the one Self (Advaita) or a liberated soul's rest in the Lord (Vishishtadvaita, Dvaita); all agree it comes with, not despite, serving all beings.

## Reflection

Whose welfare, beyond your own circle, could your effort quietly serve today?
