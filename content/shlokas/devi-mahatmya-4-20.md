## Shloka

## Word by word

- **khaḍga-prabhā-nikara-visphuraṇaiḥ** — by the flashing of the mass of your sword's brilliance
- **tathā ugraiḥ** — so fierce
- **śūla-agra-kānti-nivahena** — by the mass of radiance from the spear-tip
- **dṛśaḥ asurāṇām** — the eyes of the asuras
- **yat na āgatāḥ vilayam** — that (they) did not come to destruction
- **aṃśumat-indu-khaṇḍa-yogya-ānanam** — (your) face, fit to be likened to a piece of the radiant moon
- **tava** — your
- **vilokayatām** — of those who were gazing (at it)
- **tat etat** — that is the reason for this

## Meaning

A poet's conceit inside scripture. Weapons as bright as hers should have blinded anyone who looked, and the only reason the asuras kept their sight, the gods say, is that they were looking not at the weapons but at her face, gentle as moonlight. It is an elegant way of saying that her beauty and her terror are present in the same instant, and that even her enemies, at the end, chose to look at the beauty. Because it depends on the battle scene for its sense, it is a reader's verse rather than a daily one. The claim underneath travels well, though — what is frightening and what is lovely can arrive together.

## Meaning (hi)

शास्त्र के भीतर एक कवि की कल्पना। उनके जैसे चमकते शस्त्र देखने वाले किसी को भी अन्धा कर देते, और देवता कहते हैं कि असुरों की दृष्टि बची रहने का एकमात्र कारण यह था कि वे शस्त्रों को नहीं, उनका मुख देख रहे थे, जो चाँदनी-सा कोमल है। यह कहने का सुरुचिपूर्ण ढंग है कि उनका सौन्दर्य और भय एक ही क्षण में मौजूद हैं, और अन्त में शत्रुओं ने भी सौन्दर्य को ही देखना चुना। चूँकि इसका अर्थ युद्ध के दृश्य पर टिका है, यह पढ़ने का श्लोक है, रोज़ का नहीं। पर नीचे का दावा दूर तक जाता है — जो डरावना है और जो सुन्दर है, वे साथ-साथ आ सकते हैं।

## Reflection

When something frightening and something beautiful arrive at the same moment, which one do you look at?
