## Shloka

**Devanagari:** सर्वधर्मान्परित्यज्य मामेकं शरणं व्रज । / अहं त्वां सर्वपापेभ्यो मोक्षयिष्यामि मा शुचः ॥18.66॥
**IAST:** sarvadharmānparityajya māmekaṃ śaraṇaṃ vraja । / ahaṃ tvāṃ sarvapāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi mā śucaḥ ॥18.66॥
**Say it:** sar-va-dhar-MAAN pa-rit-YUJ-ya maa-MAY-kum SHA-ra-num VRA-ja / a-HUM tvaam sar-va-paa-PAYB-hyo mohk-sha-yish-YAA-mi maa SHOO-chah
**Meaning:** Abandoning all dharmas, come to me alone for refuge. I will free you from all sins — do not grieve.

## Word by word

- **sarva-dharmān** — all dharmas, all duties and supports
- **parityajya** — having completely let go of
- **mām ekam** — to me alone
- **śaraṇam vraja** — come for refuge
- **aham** — I
- **tvām** — you
- **sarva-pāpebhyaḥ** — from all sins
- **mokṣayiṣyāmi** — I will free, I will liberate
- **mā śucaḥ** — do not grieve

## Meaning

The Gita's final instruction, and its most debated. What "abandoning all dharmas" means genuinely divides the great commentarial traditions, and no single reading is the settled one. The Shrivaishnava tradition receives this as the charama shloka — the ultimate verse — teaching prapatti: complete surrender to the Lord as itself the way, with the divine promise doing the saving. Advaita reads the abandonment through renunciation and knowledge: the one who knows the Self stands beyond all action and its duties. Dvaita reads it differently again, as performing all dharma in surrender to Vishnu rather than discarding duty. What every reading shares is the ending: refuge is offered, the burden is lifted — mā śucaḥ, do not grieve.

## Reflection

What are you carrying alone that this verse invites you to set down and entrust?
