## Shloka

**Devanagari:** योऽन्तःसुखोऽन्तरारामस्तथान्तर्ज्योतिरेव यः । / स योगी ब्रह्मनिर्वाणं ब्रह्मभूतोऽधिगच्छति ॥5.24॥
**IAST:** yo'ntaḥsukho'ntarārāmastathāntarjyotireva yaḥ । / sa yogī brahmanirvāṇaṃ brahmabhūto'dhigacchati ॥5.24॥
**Say it:** yon-tah-SU-kho un-ta-RAA-raa-mus ta-THAAN-tar-JYO-tir AY-va yah / sa YO-gee brah-ma-nir-VAA-num brah-ma-BHOO-to dhi-GUCH-chha-ti
**Meaning:** The one whose happiness is within, whose delight is within, and whose light is within — that yogi, having become one with Brahman, attains the stillness of Brahman.

## Word by word

- **yaḥ** — who
- **antaḥ-sukhaḥ** — whose happiness is within
- **antar-ārāmaḥ** — whose delight, whose resting place, is within
- **tathā** — likewise
- **antar-jyotiḥ** — whose light is within
- **eva** — truly
- **yaḥ** — who
- **saḥ yogī** — that yogi
- **brahma-nirvāṇam** — the stillness of Brahman (brahma-nirvana)
- **brahma-bhūtaḥ** — having become Brahman, absorbed in Brahman
- **adhigacchati** — attains, reaches

## Meaning

Three times the verse says "within": happiness within, delight within, light within. It is describing someone whose sources have moved indoors — who no longer needs the day to go well, the room to approve, or circumstances to supply the glow. Such a person is not withdrawn from the world; they simply are not billing the world for their contentment. The reward named is brahma-nirvana, a settled stillness in the Divine. For daily practice, ask where you are currently outsourcing your mood, and reclaim one small piece of it. Traditions diverge honestly on "having become Brahman": Advaita reads it as realising identity with Brahman, while Vishishtadvaita and Dvaita read it as the soul reaching its purified, Brahman-like nature while remaining distinct from the Lord.

## Reflection

What would today look like if you drew your contentment from within instead of waiting for it to be delivered?
