## Shloka

**Devanagari:** पत्रं पुष्पं फलं तोयं यो मे भक्त्या प्रयच्छति । / तदहं भक्त्युपहृतमश्नामि प्रयतात्मनः ॥9.26॥
**IAST:** patraṃ puṣpaṃ phalaṃ toyaṃ yo me bhaktyā prayacchati । / tadahaṃ bhaktyupahṛtamaśnāmi prayatātmanaḥ ॥9.26॥
**Say it:** PUT-rum POOSH-pum PHA-lum TOH-yum yoh may BHAK-tyaa pra-YUCH-cha-ti / ta-DA-hum bhak-tyoo-pa-HRI-tum ush-NAA-mi pra-ya-TAAT-ma-nah
**Meaning:** Whoever offers me a leaf, a flower, a fruit, or water with devotion — that offering of love from a person of pure heart, I accept.

## Word by word

- **patram** — a leaf
- **puṣpam** — a flower
- **phalam** — a fruit
- **toyam** — water
- **yaḥ** — whoever
- **me** — to me
- **bhaktyā** — with devotion
- **prayacchati** — offers
- **tat** — that
- **aham** — I
- **bhakti-upahṛtam** — brought with devotion
- **aśnāmi** — I accept, I partake of
- **prayata-ātmanaḥ** — of one whose self is pure, disciplined

## Meaning

The verse behind the everyday offering. What reaches the divine is not the price of the gift but the devotion it is carried with — a leaf, a flower, a fruit, a cup of water are enough, and Krishna says he accepts them personally. The one qualifier is prayata-ātmanaḥ: the offerer's heart is clean, the giving sincere. This is the scriptural root of the simple home puja practised across Hindu households, where whatever is at hand becomes an offering. For daily practice it lowers every barrier: you do not need wealth, ritual expertise, or a temple. You need one small thing, given with full attention.

## Reflection

What small, ordinary thing could you offer today with your whole attention?
