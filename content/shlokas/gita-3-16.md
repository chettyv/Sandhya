## Shloka

**Devanagari:** एवं प्रवर्तितं चक्रं नानुवर्तयतीह यः । / अघायुरिन्द्रियारामो मोघं पार्थ स जीवति ॥3.16॥
**IAST:** evaṃ pravartitaṃ cakraṃ nānuvartayatīha yaḥ । / aghāyurindriyārāmo moghaṃ pārtha sa jīvati ॥3.16॥
**Say it:** AY-vum pra-var-TI-tum CHUK-rum naa-noo-var-ta-ya-TEE-ha yah / a-GHAA-yoor in-dri-YAA-raa-mo MO-ghum PAAR-tha sa JEE-va-ti
**Meaning:** One who does not help turn the wheel thus set in motion — living wrongly, absorbed in the senses — lives in vain, Partha.

## Word by word

- **evam** — thus, in this way
- **pravartitam** — set in motion
- **cakram** — the wheel (of mutual sustenance)
- **na anuvartayati** — does not keep turning, does not follow along with
- **iha** — here, in this world
- **yaḥ** — who
- **agha-āyuḥ** — whose life is sinful, wrongly lived
- **indriya-ārāmaḥ** — delighting in the senses
- **mogham** — in vain, uselessly
- **pārtha** — O Partha (Arjuna)
- **saḥ** — that person
- **jīvati** — lives

## Meaning

The "wheel" is the cycle Krishna has just described: beings live on food, food comes from rain, rain from sacrifice, sacrifice from action — a circulation of mutual giving that sustains the world. To take from that cycle without contributing, living only for one's own pleasure, is to live pointlessly. For daily practice the verse asks a blunt question: are you a participant or only a consumer? Everyone receives constantly — from family, society, nature; the verse insists something must flow back. Commentators genuinely differ on the wheel's scope — some in the Advaita line stress the Vedic ritual cycle for those not yet renunciates, while theistic readings see the whole cosmic order of offering to the Lord — but none excuses free-riding.

## Reflection

Where are you drawing from the wheel — family, community, nature — without putting anything back?
