## Word by word

- **tataḥ** — then
- **parājitāḥ devāḥ** — the defeated gods
- **padma-yonim** — the lotus-born
- **prajāpatim** — Prajapati (Brahma)
- **puraskṛtya** — placing at their head
- **gatāḥ tatra** — went there
- **yatra** — where
- **īśa** — Isha (Shiva)
- **garuḍa-dhvajau** — and the Garuda-bannered one (Vishnu) — the two of them

## Meaning

Defeat teaches the gods a procedure: they do not scatter, they organize. They choose a spokesman — Brahma, the eldest, the one whose speech carries weight — and go together to the two greatest powers they know. There is no shame in the scene; asking for help is depicted as the intelligent act of the defeated, done in order, with a leader, out loud. The text quietly assumes what the whole chapter will prove: no single power, not even Shiva or Vishnu alone, resolves this — the going-together is already the beginning of the answer.

## Meaning (hi)

पराजय देवताओं को एक विधि सिखाती है: वे बिखरते नहीं, संगठित होते हैं। वे एक प्रवक्ता चुनते हैं — ब्रह्मा, ज्येष्ठ, जिनकी वाणी का भार सब मानते हैं — और मिलकर उन दो महाशक्तियों के पास जाते हैं जिन्हें वे जानते हैं। इस दृश्य में कोई लज्जा नहीं है; सहायता माँगना यहाँ हारे हुए का बुद्धिमान कर्म है — क्रम से, एक अगुवा के साथ, खुलकर। पाठ चुपचाप वही मान लेता है जो पूरा अध्याय सिद्ध करेगा: अकेली कोई भी शक्ति — शिव या विष्णु भी — इसे नहीं सुलझाती; साथ जाना ही उत्तर की शुरुआत है।

## Reflection

When you are defeated, do you go for help deliberately and together — or scattered and alone?
