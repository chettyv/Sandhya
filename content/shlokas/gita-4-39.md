## Shloka

**Devanagari:** श्रद्धावाँल्लभते ज्ञानं तत्परः संयतेन्द्रियः । / ज्ञानं लब्ध्वा परां शान्तिमचिरेणाधिगच्छति ॥4.39॥
**IAST:** śraddhāvām̐llabhate jñānaṃ tatparaḥ saṃyatendriyaḥ । / jñānaṃ labdhvā parāṃ śāntimacireṇādhigacchati ॥4.39॥
**Say it:** shrad-DHAA-vaan LA-bha-tay GYAA-nam TAT-pa-rah sam-ya-TEN-dri-yah / GYAA-nam LAB-dhvaa pa-RAAM SHAAN-tim a-chi-RAY-naa-dhi-GACH-cha-ti
**Meaning:** The person of faith, devoted to it, with senses restrained, gains knowledge; having gained knowledge, one soon reaches the highest peace.

## Word by word

- **śraddhāvān** — the one who has faith
- **labhate** — gains, obtains
- **jñānam** — knowledge
- **tat-paraḥ** — devoted to it, intent on it
- **saṃyata-indriyaḥ** — with senses restrained
- **jñānam** — knowledge
- **labdhvā** — having gained
- **parām** — supreme, highest
- **śāntim** — peace
- **acireṇa** — before long, quickly
- **adhigacchati** — attains, reaches

## Meaning

Krishna gives the recipe in order: faith, commitment, restraint — then knowledge — then peace. Faith (shraddha) here is not blind belief but working trust, the willingness to take the teaching seriously enough to test it in your own life. Commitment means making it your priority rather than a hobby; restraint of the senses means not letting every passing pull scatter the effort. Given those three, knowledge comes — and once knowledge comes, the verse promises, peace follows without long delay. For daily practice, notice what the sequence rules out: peace is not chased directly. Chase understanding with trust and steadiness, and peace arrives as the by-product it was always meant to be.

## Reflection

Which of the three — trust, commitment, or restraint — is the weakest link in your practice right now?
