## Shloka

**Devanagari:** श्रेयान्द्रव्यमयाद्यज्ञाज्ज्ञानयज्ञः परन्तप । / सर्वं कर्माखिलं पार्थ ज्ञाने परिसमाप्यते ॥4.33॥
**IAST:** śreyāndravyamayādyajñājjñānayajñaḥ parantapa । / sarvaṃ karmākhilaṃ pārtha jñāne parisamāpyate ॥4.33॥
**Say it:** SHRAY-yaan drav-ya-ma-YAAD yag-YAAJ gyaa-na-YAG-yah pa-ran-TA-pa / SAR-vam kar-MAA-khi-lam PAAR-tha GYAA-nay pa-ri-sa-MAAP-ya-tay
**Meaning:** Better than the sacrifice of material things is the sacrifice of knowledge, O scorcher of foes; all action without exception, O Partha, culminates in knowledge.

## Word by word

- **śreyān** — better
- **dravyamayāt** — than that made of material things
- **yajñāt** — than sacrifice
- **jñāna-yajñaḥ** — the sacrifice of knowledge
- **parantapa** — O scorcher of foes (Arjuna)
- **sarvam** — all
- **karma** — action
- **akhilam** — without exception, entire
- **pārtha** — O Partha (Arjuna)
- **jñāne** — in knowledge
- **parisamāpyate** — culminates, reaches completion

## Meaning

After listing many kinds of sacrifice — offerings of wealth, austerity, breath, study — Krishna ranks them: the offering made in knowledge outweighs any offering of things. Not because giving things is wrong, but because outer ritual finds its purpose in inner understanding; all action, without exception, ripens into knowledge. For daily practice, this reorders effort. Doing more is not automatically better than understanding why you do it. Bring understanding into the act — know what your work is for, what your worship means, what your giving serves — and the same action becomes a higher sacrifice. Ritual done blindly stays external; ritual done knowingly transforms the one performing it.

## Reflection

What is one routine you perform on autopilot that would change if you remembered why you do it?
