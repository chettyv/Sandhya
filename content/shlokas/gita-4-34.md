## Shloka

**Devanagari:** तद्विद्धि प्रणिपातेन परिप्रश्नेन सेवया । / उपदेक्ष्यन्ति ते ज्ञानं ज्ञानिनस्तत्त्वदर्शिनः ॥4.34॥
**IAST:** tadviddhi praṇipātena paripraśnena sevayā । / upadekṣyanti te jñānaṃ jñāninastattvadarśinaḥ ॥4.34॥
**Say it:** tad-VID-dhi pra-ni-PAA-tay-na pa-ri-PRASH-nay-na SAY-va-yaa / oo-pa-DEK-shyan-ti tay GYAA-nam GYAA-ni-nas tat-tva-DAR-shi-nah
**Meaning:** Learn that knowledge by bowing, by sincere questioning, and by service; the wise who have seen the truth will teach it to you.

## Word by word

- **tat** — that (knowledge)
- **viddhi** — know, learn
- **praṇipātena** — by prostration, by humble approach
- **paripraśnena** — by thorough questioning
- **sevayā** — by service
- **upadekṣyanti** — they will teach
- **te** — to you
- **jñānam** — knowledge
- **jñāninaḥ** — the knowers
- **tattva-darśinaḥ** — seers of the truth

## Meaning

The classic verse on how spiritual knowledge is actually transmitted. Krishna gives three requirements, and all three are attitudes of the student, not qualifications of the teacher: humility (bowing — being teachable), inquiry (asking real questions, not performing knowledge), and service (staying long enough, and giving enough, for trust to form). Notice the verse does not say knowledge is downloaded; it says the truth-seers will teach you when approached this way. For daily practice: pick your teachers carefully — the standard here is that they have seen the truth, not that they are popular — and then check your own posture. Arrogance closes the very door that questions are meant to open.

## Reflection

Whom do you trust enough to ask a real question — and when did you last actually ask one?
