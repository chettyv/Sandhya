## Shloka

**Devanagari:** नखानामुद्द्योतैर्नवनलिनरागं विहसतां / कराणां ते कान्तिं कथय कथयामः कथमुमे । / कयाचिद्वा साम्यं भजतु कलया हन्त कमलं / यदि क्रीडल्लक्ष्मीचरणतललाक्षारुणदलम् ॥71॥

<!-- review note: No conversion artifacts spotted. The final pāda circulates in more than one form in printed editions; लाक्षारुणदलम् ("petals reddened by lac-dye") is a well-attested reading and scans correctly. Left as the source has it. -->

**IAST:** nakhānāmuddyotairnavanalinarāgaṃ vihasatāṃ / karāṇāṃ te kāntiṃ kathaya kathayāmaḥ kathamume । / kayācidvā sāmyaṃ bhajatu kalayā hanta kamalaṃ / yadi krīḍallakṣmīcaraṇatalalākṣāruṇadalam ॥71॥
**Say it:** na-khaa-naam ud-DYO-tair na-va-na-li-na-RAA-gum vi-ha-sa-taam / ka-RAA-naam tay KAAN-tim KA-tha-ya ka-tha-YAA-mah KA-tham u-may / ka-yaa-CHID vaa SAAM-yum BHA-ja-tu ka-la-yaa HAN-ta ka-ma-lum / YA-di kree-dal-laksh-mee-cha-ra-na-ta-la-LAAK-shaa-ru-na-da-lam
**Meaning:** Tell us yourself, O Uma — how could we describe the radiance of your hands, whose flashing nails laugh at the blush of a fresh-blown lotus? Perhaps, alas, the lotus might approach some fraction of their likeness — but only if its petals were reddened by lac-dye from the soles of Lakshmi's feet as she plays upon it.
**Meaning (hi):** हे उमा, आप ही बताइए — आपके हाथों की उस कान्ति का वर्णन हम कैसे करें, जिनके नखों की चमक ताज़े खिले कमल की ललाई की हँसी उड़ाती है? हाय, कमल शायद उनकी किसी एक कला की बराबरी कर भी ले — पर तभी, जब उस पर क्रीड़ा करती लक्ष्मी के तलवों का महावर उसकी पंखुड़ियों को रँग दे।
**Source:** Soundarya Lahari 71 (traditionally attributed to Adi Shankaracharya), sanskritdocuments.org ITRANS file, personal-study terms, https://sanskritdocuments.org/doc_devii/soundaryalahari.itx

## Word by word

- **nakhānām uddyotaiḥ** — with the flashes of their nails
- **nava-nalina-rāgam** — the blush of a fresh-blown lotus
- **vihasatām** — laughing at, outshining
- **karāṇām te** — of your hands
- **kāntim** — the radiance
- **kathaya** — you tell us
- **kathayāmaḥ katham** — how are we to tell it?
- **ume** — O Uma
- **kayācit kalayā** — by some small fraction
- **vā sāmyam bhajatu** — might perhaps attain likeness
- **hanta** — alas!
- **kamalam** — the lotus
- **yadi** — only if
- **krīḍat-lakṣmī** — Lakshmi at play (in the lotus, her home)
- **caraṇa-tala-lākṣā** — the lac-dye of the soles of her feet
- **aruṇa-dalam** — its petals turned red

## Meaning

The stock comparison for beautiful hands is the pink lotus; this verse takes the cliché apart with a jeweller's patience. The Devi's nails alone out-glow the flower, so the lotus fails at the first step. Could anything rescue the comparison? Only an absurdly lovely scenario: Lakshmi, who dwells in the lotus, would have to dance across its petals until the red lac of her soles stained them — divinity's own cosmetics recruited to help a flower keep up. The rhetorical surrender — "you tell us, Uma; we cannot" — is itself the praise. When language admits defeat this gracefully, the admission becomes the poem.

## Meaning (hi)

सुन्दर हाथों की घिसी-पिटी उपमा है गुलाबी कमल; यह श्लोक उस उपमा को जौहरी के धीरज से खोलता है। देवी के नख ही फूल से अधिक दमकते हैं, तो कमल पहली ही सीढ़ी पर हार जाता है। उपमा बच सकती है क्या? बस एक असम्भव-सा सुन्दर दृश्य हो तो: कमल-वासिनी लक्ष्मी उसकी पंखुड़ियों पर तब तक क्रीड़ा करें जब तक उनके तलवों का महावर उन्हें रँग न दे — फूल की बराबरी करवाने के लिए देवत्व का शृंगार उधार लेना पड़े। और 'हे उमा, आप ही कहिए; हम नहीं कह सकते' — यह वाक्-हार ही असली स्तुति है। भाषा जब इतनी शालीनता से हार माने, तो हार ही कविता बन जाती है।
