## Shloka

**Devanagari:** यस्तु विज्ञानवान्भवति युक्तेन मनसा सदा । / तस्येन्द्रियाणि वश्यानि सदश्वा इव सारथेः ॥1.3.6॥

**IAST:** yastu vijñānavānbhavati yuktena manasā sadā । / tasyendriyāṇi vaśyāni sadaśvā iva sāratheḥ ॥1.3.6॥

**Meaning:** But whoever has understanding, whose mind is always held like reins in hand — his senses obey, like the good horses of a charioteer.

## Word by word

- **yaḥ tu** — but whoever
- **vijñānavān** — possessed of discernment, understanding
- **bhavati** — is
- **yuktena manasā** — with a yoked, disciplined mind
- **sadā** — always
- **tasya** — his
- **indriyāṇi** — senses
- **vaśyāni** — controllable, obedient
- **sadaśvāḥ iva** — like good horses
- **sāratheḥ** — of a charioteer

## Meaning

The counter-image: same chariot, same horses, different outcome. When discernment is awake and the mind is held like reins in a steady hand, the senses do not need to be broken or starved — they become good horses, powerful and cooperative, carrying the traveller where the traveller actually wants to go. The Upanishad's psychology is notably kind to the senses: nothing here calls them enemies. Untrained, they scatter; yoked — yukta, the word behind "yoga" — they serve. Read with 1.3.5, the pair asks a single question of every reader: who is holding the reins?

## Meaning (hi)

अब उलट चित्र: वही रथ, वही घोड़े, पर परिणाम अलग। जब विवेक जागा हो और मन लगाम की तरह स्थिर हाथ में हो, तो इंद्रियों को तोड़ने या भूखा रखने की ज़रूरत नहीं — वे अच्छे घोड़े बन जाती हैं: शक्तिशाली, सहयोगी, यात्री को वहीं ले जाती हुईं जहाँ वह सचमुच जाना चाहता है। उपनिषद की दृष्टि इंद्रियों के प्रति उदार है: यहाँ कोई उन्हें शत्रु नहीं कहता। बिना जुते वे बिखरती हैं; "युक्त" होकर — यही शब्द "योग" की जड़ है — वे सेवा करती हैं। 1.3.5 के साथ पढ़ने पर पूरी जोड़ी बस एक सवाल पूछती है: लगाम किसके हाथ में है?

## Reflection

Which one rein — sleep, food, the phone — could you quietly take back in hand this week?
