## Shloka

**Devanagari:** नित्योऽनित्यानां चेतनश्चेतनानाम् / एको बहूनां यो विदधाति कामान् । / तमात्मस्थं येऽनुपश्यन्ति धीराः / तेषां शान्तिः शाश्वती नेतरेषाम् ॥2.2.13॥

<!-- review note: the avagraha in नित्योऽनित्यानां is genuine (nityaḥ + anityānām, "the eternal among the impermanent"). The Shvetashvatara parallel (6.13) reads नित्यो नित्यानां ("the eternal among eternals") instead — both readings are attested; left exactly as extracted. -->

**IAST:** nityo'nityānāṃ cetanaścetanānām / eko bahūnāṃ yo vidadhāti kāmān । / tamātmasthaṃ ye'nupaśyanti dhīrāḥ / teṣāṃ śāntiḥ śāśvatī netareṣām ॥2.2.13॥
**Say it:** NIT-yo-nit-YAA-naam CHAY-ta-nash-chay-ta-NAA-naam / AY-ko ba-HOO-naam yo vi-da-DHAA-ti KAA-maan / tum-aat-mus-THUM yay-nu-PASH-yun-ti DHEE-raah / TAY-shaam SHAAN-tih shaash-va-TEE NAY-ta-ray-shaam
**Meaning:** The eternal among things that pass, the conscious among the conscious, the One among the many who provides for their desires — the wise who see him abiding in themselves, theirs is lasting peace; it belongs to no one else.
**Meaning (hi):** अनित्यों के बीच नित्य, चेतनों के बीच चेतन, अनेकों के बीच वह एक — जो सबकी कामनाएँ पूरी करता है; जो धीर उसे अपने भीतर बसा देखते हैं, शाश्वत शान्ति उन्हीं की है — औरों की नहीं।

## Word by word

- **nityaḥ** — the eternal
- **anityānām** — among the impermanent
- **cetanaḥ** — the conscious
- **cetanānām** — among conscious beings
- **ekaḥ** — the one
- **bahūnām** — among the many
- **yaḥ vidadhāti** — who provides, allots
- **kāmān** — desires, their fulfilment
- **tam ātma-stham** — him, abiding in oneself
- **ye anupaśyanti dhīrāḥ** — the wise who keep seeing
- **teṣām** — theirs
- **śāntiḥ śāśvatī** — lasting peace
- **na itareṣām** — not others'

## Meaning

Twin to the previous verse, with two changes that matter. "Happiness" deepens into "peace" — śāntiḥ śāśvatī — and the One is given a job: vidadhāti kāmān, he provides for the desires of the many. The opening compounds are the engine: eternal-among-fleeting, conscious-among-conscious, one-among-many. Dvaita and Vishishtadvaita read that middle phrase literally — many real conscious selves, and one supreme Conscious One sustaining them — while Advaita reads the many consciousnesses as the One appearing in many reflectors; the verse itself holds the paradox open. A near-twin appears at Shvetashvatara 6.13, and it is among the most quoted lines in all of Vedanta. Its promise is the quietest in the Upanishad: see that One within, and peace stops being weather.

## Meaning (hi)

पिछले श्लोक का जुड़वाँ, दो बदलावों के साथ जो मायने रखते हैं। "सुख" गहराकर "शान्ति" हो जाता है — शान्तिः शाश्वती — और उस एक को एक काम दे दिया गया है: विदधाति कामान् — वही अनेकों की कामनाएँ पूरी करता है। शुरुआती समास ही श्लोक का इंजन हैं: अनित्यों में नित्य, चेतनों में चेतन, अनेकों में एक। द्वैत और विशिष्टाद्वैत बीच वाले पद को शब्दशः पढ़ते हैं — अनेक वास्तविक चेतन जीव, और उन्हें धारण करने वाला एक परम चेतन — जबकि अद्वैत अनेक चेतनाओं को अनेक दर्पणों में झलकता एक ही मानता है; श्लोक स्वयं इस पहेली को खुला रखता है। इसका जुड़वाँ श्वेताश्वतर 6.13 में मिलता है, और यह पूरे वेदान्त की सबसे अधिक उद्धृत पंक्तियों में है। वादा सबसे शान्त है: उस एक को भीतर देखो, और शान्ति मौसम नहीं रह जाती।

## Reflection

What would change today if you treated peace as something to see, not something to arrange?
