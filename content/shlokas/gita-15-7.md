## Shloka

**Devanagari:** ममैवांशो जीवलोके जीवभूतः सनातनः । / मनःषष्ठानीन्द्रियाणि प्रकृतिस्थानि कर्षति ॥15.7॥
**IAST:** mamaivāṃśo jīvaloke jīvabhūtaḥ sanātanaḥ । / manaḥṣaṣṭhānīndriyāṇi prakṛtisthāni karṣati ॥15.7॥
**Say it:** ma-my-VAAM-sho jee-va-LOH-kay jee-va-BHOO-tah sa-NAA-ta-nah / ma-nah-shush-TAA-neen-dri-YAA-ni pra-kri-ti-STAA-ni KAR-sha-ti
**Meaning:** An eternal part of my very self, having become the living soul in the world of the living, draws to itself the senses, with the mind as the sixth, which rest in nature.

## Word by word

- **mama eva** — of my very self
- **aṃśaḥ** — a part, a portion
- **jīva-loke** — in the world of the living
- **jīva-bhūtaḥ** — having become the living soul
- **sanātanaḥ** — eternal
- **manaḥ-ṣaṣṭhāni** — with the mind as the sixth
- **indriyāṇi** — the senses
- **prakṛti-sthāni** — resting in nature (prakṛti)
- **karṣati** — draws, pulls (to itself)

## Meaning

A statement of identity before anything else: the living soul in you is an eternal portion of the divine — not a product of matter, not an accident. That portion, embodied, gathers up the five senses and the mind as a sixth instrument, all borrowed from nature, and works through them. How literally to take "portion" is a genuine dividing line between schools: Advaita reads the part-language as a manner of speaking about the one undivided Self, while Vishishtadvaita and Dvaita hold the individual soul to be really distinct and eternally related to God. All agree on the practical weight: what looks out through your eyes is older and more dignified than any of the day's troubles.

## Reflection

If the one looking out through your senses is an eternal portion of the divine, how does that change what today's problems weigh?
