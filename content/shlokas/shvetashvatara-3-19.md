## Shloka

**Devanagari:** अपाणिपादो जवनो ग्रहीता पश्यत्यचक्षुः स शृणोत्यकर्णः । / स वेत्ति वेद्यं न च तस्यास्ति वेत्ता तमाहुरग्र्यं पुरुषं महान्तम् ॥3.19॥

**Meaning:** Without hands or feet, he is swift and he grasps; he sees without eyes, he hears without ears. He knows all that can be known, but of him there is no knower. They call him the primal, great Person.

## Word by word

- **apāṇi-pādaḥ** — without hands or feet
- **javanaḥ** — swift
- **grahītā** — a grasper, one who seizes
- **paśyati acakṣuḥ** — sees without eyes
- **saḥ śṛṇoti akarṇaḥ** — he hears without ears
- **saḥ vetti vedyam** — he knows the knowable
- **na ca tasya asti vettā** — but of him there is no knower
- **tam āhuḥ** — him they call
- **agryam** — foremost, primal
- **puruṣam mahāntam** — the great Person

## Meaning

The mirror-image of 3.16: there, hands and feet everywhere; here, none at all — and still he moves, holds, sees, hears. Read together, the two verses teach how the hymn wants its own imagery held: every limb was metaphor, and so is every absence. The sharpest line is the epistemic one — he knows whatever can be known, but of him there is no knower: he can never be turned around and made an object. Advaita presses this hard: the knower is what you are, never something you catch sight of. The devotional schools read the same words as the Lord's unfathomable depth. Then the hymn simply bows: the primal, great Person.

## Meaning (hi)

यह 3.16 का दर्पण-चित्र है: वहाँ हर ओर हाथ-पैर; यहाँ एक भी नहीं — फिर भी वह चलता है, थामता है, देखता-सुनता है। दोनों श्लोक साथ पढ़ने पर सिखाते हैं कि स्तोत्र अपनी ही उपमाएँ कैसे पकड़वाना चाहता है: हर अंग रूपक था, और हर अनुपस्थिति भी। सबसे धारदार पंक्ति ज्ञान की है — वह हर जानने-योग्य को जानता है, पर उसे जानने वाला कोई नहीं: उसे घुमाकर कभी विषय नहीं बनाया जा सकता। अद्वैत इसे पूरे दबाव से पढ़ता है: ज्ञाता वह है जो तुम हो, वह नहीं जो तुम्हें दिख जाए। भक्ति-शाखाएँ इन्हीं शब्दों में प्रभु की अथाह गहराई पढ़ती हैं। फिर स्तोत्र बस प्रणाम करता है: आद्य, महान पुरुष।

## Reflection

What do you know today not as an object in front of you but as the very one who is looking?
