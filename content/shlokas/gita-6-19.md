## Shloka

**Devanagari:** यथा दीपो निवातस्थो नेङ्गते सोपमा स्मृता । / योगिनो यतचित्तस्य युञ्जतो योगमात्मनः ॥6.19॥
**IAST:** yathā dīpo nivātastho neṅgate sopamā smṛtā । / yogino yatacittasya yuñjato yogamātmanaḥ ॥6.19॥
**Say it:** ya-THAA DEE-po ni-VAA-tus-tho NENG-ga-tay so-pa-MAA smri-TAA / YO-gi-no ya-ta-CHIT-tus-ya YOON-ja-to yo-ga-MAAT-ma-naha
**Meaning:** As a lamp standing in a windless place does not flicker — that is the simile recalled for the yogi of restrained mind, practising the yoga of the self.

## Word by word

- **yathā** — just as
- **dīpaḥ** — a lamp
- **nivāta-sthaḥ** — standing in a windless place
- **na iṅgate** — does not flicker
- **sā upamā** — that simile
- **smṛtā** — is traditionally recalled
- **yoginaḥ** — of the yogi
- **yata-cittasya** — whose mind is restrained
- **yuñjataḥ** — practising, engaging in
- **yogam** — the yoga
- **ātmanaḥ** — of the self

## Meaning

One of the Gita's most loved images: a lamp flame in a windless place does not flicker — that, the verse says, is the traditional simile for the mind of a disciplined yogi absorbed in the yoga of the self. The image teaches by contrast with our ordinary state, where every draught of thought, worry, and news bends the flame. Notice what the simile does not promise: the wind is not abolished; the lamp is simply placed where wind cannot reach it. Daily practice is largely the craft of building that windless place — a regular time, a seat, a closed door — and then letting the flame stand still on its own.

What would a windless place look like in your day — and when could you sit in it?
