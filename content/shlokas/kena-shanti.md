## Shloka

**Devanagari:** ॐ आप्यायन्तु ममाङ्गानि वाक्प्राणश्चक्षुः / श्रोत्रमथो बलमिन्द्रियाणि च सर्वाणि । / सर्वं ब्रह्मौपनिषदं / माऽहं ब्रह्म निराकुर्यां मा मा ब्रह्म / निराकरोदनिराकरणमस्त्वनिराकरणं मेऽस्तु । / तदात्मनि निरते य / उपनिषत्सु धर्मास्ते मयि सन्तु ते मयि सन्तु । / ॐ शान्तिः शान्तिः शान्तिः

**Meaning:** May my limbs grow strong, and my speech, breath, eye, ear, and all my senses gain strength. All this is the Brahman of the Upanishads. May I never deny Brahman; may Brahman never deny me. Let there be no rejection — let there at least be no rejection from my side. May the truths that live in the Upanishads live in me, who delight in the Self. Om, peace, peace, peace.

## Word by word

- **āpyāyantu** — may they grow strong, be nourished
- **mama aṅgāni** — my limbs
- **vāk** — speech
- **prāṇaḥ** — breath, life-force
- **cakṣuḥ** — eye
- **śrotram** — ear
- **atho** — and also
- **balam** — strength
- **indriyāṇi ca sarvāṇi** — and all the senses
- **sarvam** — all this
- **brahma aupaniṣadam** — is the Brahman taught in the Upanishads
- **mā aham brahma nirākuryām** — may I not deny Brahman
- **mā mā brahma nirākarot** — may Brahman not deny me
- **anirākaraṇam astu** — let there be no denial
- **anirākaraṇam me astu** — let there be no denial on my part
- **tad-ātmani nirate** — in me, who delight in that Self
- **ye upaniṣatsu dharmāḥ** — the truths that are in the Upanishads
- **te mayi santu** — may they dwell in me
- **śāntiḥ** — peace

## Meaning

Before the questions begin, the student prays for a fit instrument: healthy limbs, clear senses, a mind strong enough to carry what it is about to hear. Then comes the striking double vow — may I never turn away from Brahman, and may Brahman never turn away from me — as if knowledge were a relationship both sides must keep. This shanti mantra is recited before study of the Kena across traditions; schools describe the Brahman it invokes differently, but the prayer itself is shared. It ends with peace spoken three times — for disturbances arising from oneself, from other beings, and from nature.

## Meaning (hi)

प्रश्न शुरू होने से पहले शिष्य एक योग्य साधन की प्रार्थना करता है: स्वस्थ अंग, स्पष्ट इंद्रियाँ, और इतना बलवान मन जो सुनी हुई बात को धारण कर सके। फिर आता है वह विलक्षण दोहरा व्रत — मैं ब्रह्म से कभी न मुड़ूँ, और ब्रह्म मुझसे कभी न मुड़े — मानो ज्ञान एक ऐसा रिश्ता हो जिसे दोनों पक्षों को निभाना है। यह शांति मंत्र सभी परंपराओं में केन उपनिषद के अध्ययन से पहले पढ़ा जाता है; ब्रह्म के स्वरूप को परंपराएँ अलग-अलग समझाती हैं, पर प्रार्थना सबकी साझी है। अंत में तीन बार शांति कही जाती है — अपने भीतर से, दूसरे प्राणियों से और प्रकृति से आने वाले विघ्नों के लिए।

## Reflection

Before you learn anything today, what would it mean to ask that you not turn away from the truth — and that it not turn away from you?
