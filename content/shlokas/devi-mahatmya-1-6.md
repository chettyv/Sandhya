## Shloka

## Word by word

- **tataḥ** — then
- **sva-puram āyātaḥ** — having come back to his own city
- **nija-deśa-adhipaḥ abhavat** — he became ruler of only his own country
- **ākrāntaḥ** — attacked, overrun
- **saḥ mahābhāgaḥ** — that illustrious one
- **taiḥ tadā** — by them, then
- **prabala-aribhiḥ** — by the powerful enemies

## Meaning

The loss comes in stages, the way real losses do. From emperor of the whole earth, Suratha shrinks to king of one country — and even that remnant is not left in peace. The storyteller understands something about catastrophe: it rarely arrives as a single blow. Each time the king retreats to a smaller version of his life, the trouble follows him in. Notice the text still calls him mahābhāga, "illustrious, greatly fortunate" — his worth has not changed, only his circumstances. The gap between those two things is exactly what the sage will later ask him to see.

## Meaning (hi)

हानि क़िस्तों में आती है, जैसे असल ज़िन्दगी में आती है। समस्त पृथ्वी के सम्राट से सुरथ सिमटकर एक देश के राजा रह जाते हैं — और वह बचा हुआ हिस्सा भी चैन से नहीं रहने दिया जाता। कथाकार विपत्ति का स्वभाव जानता है: वह प्रायः एक ही वार में नहीं आती। राजा जब-जब अपने जीवन के किसी छोटे संस्करण में सिमटता है, संकट पीछे-पीछे भीतर चला आता है। ध्यान दीजिए — पाठ उन्हें अब भी "महाभाग" कहता है: उनका मूल्य नहीं बदला, केवल परिस्थितियाँ बदली हैं। इन्हीं दोनों के बीच का अन्तर आगे ऋषि उन्हें दिखाएँगे।

## Reflection

When your circumstances shrank, did you let your sense of your own worth shrink with them?
