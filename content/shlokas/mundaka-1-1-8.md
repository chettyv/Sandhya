## Shloka

**Devanagari:** तपसा चीयते ब्रह्म ततोऽन्नमभिजायते । / अन्नात् प्राणो मनः सत्यं लोकाः कर्मसु चामृतम् ॥1.1.8॥

**IAST:** tapasā cīyate brahma tato'nnamabhijāyate । / annāt prāṇo manaḥ satyaṃ lokāḥ karmasu cāmṛtam ॥1.1.8॥
**Say it:** ta-pa-SAA CHEE-ya-tay BRAH-ma ta-TON-num a-bhi-JAA-ya-tay / an-NAAT praa-NO ma-NAH sat-YUM lo-KAAH kar-ma-SU chaam-RI-tum
**Meaning:** By tapas Brahman swells; from that, food is born; and from food — life-breath, mind, the real, the worlds, and in works, the deathless fruit.

## Word by word

- **tapasā** — by tapas, by brooding heat of concentration
- **cīyate** — swells, builds up
- **brahma** — Brahman (here, the creative power about to unfold)
- **tataḥ** — from that
- **annam** — food, nourishing matter
- **abhijāyate** — is born
- **annāt** — from food
- **prāṇaḥ** — life-breath
- **manaḥ** — mind
- **satyam** — the real, the ordered world
- **lokāḥ** — the worlds
- **karmasu** — in works, in rites
- **ca amṛtam** — and the deathless (fruit)

## Meaning

Creation told as incubation. Tapas is the heat of a bird on its egg, of attention held in one place until something quickens — and by it, the text says, Brahman "swells" into world-bearing fullness. Then the chain runs: heat, food, breath, mind, the ordered real, the worlds, and finally the lasting fruit that follows works. The commentators differ, and say so: Shankara takes the swelling brahman here as the first-born creative power, Hiranyagarbha, not the supreme itself, while other readings keep the supreme in view throughout — a genuine divergence with no umpire. Either way, the verse's working claim survives translation: before anything exists, there is intensity of attention.

## Meaning (hi)

सृष्टि की कथा, सेने की तरह कही गई। तप वह ऊष्मा है जो चिड़िया अंडे को देती है — एक जगह टिकाया गया ध्यान, जब तक कुछ जी न उठे — और उससे, ग्रंथ कहता है, ब्रह्म "बढ़ता" है, जगत धारण करने लायक़ पूर्णता तक। फिर शृंखला चलती है: ऊष्मा, अन्न, प्राण, मन, सुव्यवस्थित सत्य, लोक, और अंत में कर्मों के पीछे आने वाला स्थायी फल। भाष्यकार यहाँ अलग पढ़ते हैं, और खुलकर: शंकर इस बढ़ते ब्रह्म को प्रथमजात सृजन-शक्ति हिरण्यगर्भ मानते हैं, परब्रह्म नहीं; दूसरे पाठ परम को ही आद्यंत रखते हैं — एक सच्चा मतभेद, बिना निर्णायक के। जो भी हो, श्लोक का काम का दावा अनुवाद में बच जाता है: कुछ भी होने से पहले, ध्यान की तीव्रता होती है।

## Reflection

What in your life is waiting for the slow heat of sustained attention before it can be born?
