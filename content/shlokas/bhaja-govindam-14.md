## Shloka

**Devanagari:** जटिलो मुण्डी लुञ्छितकेशः / काषायाम्बरबहुकृतवेषः । / पश्यन्नपि च न पश्यति मूढो / ह्युदरनिमित्तं बहुकृतवेषः ॥14॥

<!-- review note: No conversion artifacts spotted. Printed editions vary between लुञ्छित and लुञ्चित (luñchita/luñcita, "plucked"); left as the source has it. The repetition of बहुकृतवेषः as the fourth pāda's ending is the received reading in this recension, not a copy slip. -->

**IAST:** jaṭilo muṇḍī luñchitakeśaḥ / kāṣāyāmbarabahukṛtaveṣaḥ । / paśyannapi ca na paśyati mūḍho / hyudaranimittaṃ bahukṛtaveṣaḥ ॥14॥
**Say it:** JA-ti-lo MOON-dee LOON-chi-ta-KAY-shah / kaa-SHAA-yaam-ba-ra-BA-hu-kri-ta-VAY-shah / PASH-yan-na-pi cha na PASH-ya-ti MOO-dho / HYU-da-ra-ni-MIT-tum BA-hu-kri-ta-VAY-shah
**Meaning:** One wears matted locks, one a shaven head, one hair plucked out, another ochre robes — costume upon costume. The fool sees and yet does not see: all this dressing-up is for the belly's sake.
**Meaning (hi):** कोई जटाधारी, कोई मुण्डित, कोई केश नुचवाए, कोई गेरुए वस्त्रों में — वेश पर वेश। मूढ़ देखकर भी नहीं देखता: यह सारा स्वांग तो पेट के लिए रचा गया है।
**Source:** Bhaja Govindam 14 (attributed to Adi Shankaracharya and his disciples), sanskritdocuments.org ITRANS file, personal-study terms, https://sanskritdocuments.org/doc_vishhnu/bhajagovindam.itx

## Word by word

- **jaṭilaḥ** — one with matted hair
- **muṇḍī** — one with a shaven head
- **luñchita-keśaḥ** — one whose hair is plucked out
- **kāṣāya-ambara** — ochre robes
- **bahukṛta-veṣaḥ** — wearing many a guise
- **paśyan api ca** — even while seeing
- **na paśyati** — does not see
- **mūḍhaḥ** — the fool
- **hi** — indeed
- **udara-nimittam** — for the sake of the belly
- **bahukṛta-veṣaḥ** — (all this) elaborately made costume

## Meaning

With this stanza the poem turns its hammer on religion itself. The costumes named are an even-handed survey of the renunciant world — matted locks, shaven crowns, plucked hair, ochre cloth — and the critique lands on none of the orders as such. It lands on the wearer for whom the uniform has become the livelihood: dressing as a seeker because seeking no longer pays attention, only the belly does. "Seeing, he does not see" cuts two ways — the imposter does not see his own bad faith, and onlookers do not see past the cloth. From the poem traditionally attributed to a monastic founder, this is striking honesty: robes certify nothing.

## Meaning (hi)

इस छन्द के साथ कविता का हथौड़ा धर्म पर ही उतरता है। गिनाए गए वेश त्यागी-जगत का निष्पक्ष सर्वेक्षण हैं — जटाएँ, मुण्डित सिर, नुचे केश, गेरुआ वस्त्र — और चोट किसी सम्प्रदाय पर नहीं पड़ती। चोट उस पहनने वाले पर पड़ती है जिसके लिए वर्दी रोज़गार बन गई है: साधक का वेश इसलिए, क्योंकि साधना पर नहीं, पेट पर ध्यान है। "देखता हुआ भी नहीं देखता" — यह दोनों ओर काटता है: पाखण्डी अपना खोखलापन नहीं देखता, और देखने वाले कपड़े के पार नहीं देखते। जिस रचना की परम्परागत श्रेय-पंक्ति एक मठ-संस्थापक तक जाती है, उसमें यह स्पष्टवादिता उल्लेखनीय है: वस्त्र किसी बात का प्रमाणपत्र नहीं।

## Reflection

Where does your own practice have a costume — something visible you maintain in place of the seeing it was meant to serve?
