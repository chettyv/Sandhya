## Shloka

**Devanagari:** यतश्चोदेति सूर्योऽस्तं यत्र च गच्छति । / तं देवाः सर्वेऽर्पितास्तदु नात्येति कश्चन । / एतद्वै तत् ॥2.1.9॥

**IAST:** yataścodeti sūryo'staṃ yatra ca gacchati । / taṃ devāḥ sarve'rpitāstadu nātyeti kaścana । / etadvai tat ॥2.1.9॥
**Say it:** ya-tush-cho-DAY-ti SOOR-yo-stum YAT-ra cha GACH-chha-ti / tum DAY-vaah sar-VAYR-pi-taas ta-doo NAAT-yay-ti KASH-cha-na / ay-TAD-vye tat
**Meaning:** That from which the sun rises and into which it sets — on it all the gods are fixed, and none whatsoever goes beyond it. This, truly, is That.

## Word by word

- **yataḥ** — from which
- **ca udeti** — and rises
- **sūryaḥ** — the sun
- **astam** — to its setting
- **yatra ca gacchati** — and where it goes
- **tam** — on that
- **devāḥ sarve** — all the gods
- **arpitāḥ** — are fixed, fastened
- **tat u** — that, indeed
- **na atyeti** — does not go beyond
- **kaścana** — anyone at all
- **etat vai tat** — this, truly, is That

## Meaning

From the hearth to the horizon in one step: the same reality is now shown as the ground of sunrise and sunset. That from which the sun climbs each morning and into which it settles each evening; on it all the gods rest, fixed like spokes on a hub — and nothing, no power in any world, passes beyond it. The verse is Yama's answer to a boy who asked what remains when everything else stops. The refrain lands with the calm of arithmetic: whatever rises must rise from somewhere, and whatever rules must stand on something. That ground — this, truly, is That.

## Meaning (hi)

चूल्हे से क्षितिज तक एक ही छलाँग: वही सत्ता अब सूर्योदय और सूर्यास्त की भूमि बनकर दिखाई गई है। जिससे सूर्य हर सुबह चढ़ता है और जिसमें हर शाम ढल जाता है; उसी पर सारे देवता टिके हैं, जैसे पहिये की तीलियाँ नाभि पर — और कोई भी शक्ति, किसी भी लोक की, उसके पार नहीं जाती। यह उस लड़के के प्रश्न का यम का उत्तर है जिसने पूछा था कि सब कुछ थम जाने पर क्या शेष रहता है। टेक गणित-सी शांति से उतरती है: जो उगता है, वह कहीं से उगता है; जो शासन करता है, वह किसी आधार पर खड़ा होता है। वह आधार — यही तो वह है।

## Reflection

What would you build your days around if you took seriously that nothing — not even the sun — goes beyond That?
