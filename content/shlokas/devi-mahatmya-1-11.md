## Shloka

**Devanagari:** सोऽचिन्तयत् तदा तत्र ममत्वाकृष्टचेतनः । / मत्पूर्वैः पालितं पूर्वं मया हीनं पुरं हि तत् । / मद्भृत्यैस्तैरसद्वृत्तैर्धर्मतः पाल्यते न वा ॥1.11॥

**Meaning:** Then, there, his mind dragged along by the sense of "mine", he brooded: "That city my forefathers guarded before me is now without me. Are those ill-behaved servants of mine protecting it rightly — or not?"

## Word by word

- **saḥ acintayat** — he thought, brooded
- **tadā tatra** — then, there
- **mamatva-ākṛṣṭa-cetanaḥ** — his mind dragged by the sense of "mine"
- **mat-pūrvaiḥ** — by my forefathers
- **pālitam pūrvam** — guarded before
- **mayā hīnam** — bereft of me
- **puram hi tat** — that city indeed
- **mad-bhṛtyaiḥ taiḥ** — by those servants of mine
- **asad-vṛttaiḥ** — ill-conducted
- **dharmataḥ pālyate na vā** — is it rightly protected, or not?

## Meaning

Here is the king's inner monologue, and the text diagnoses it before he speaks: his mind is ākṛṣṭa, dragged, by mamatva — the sense of "mine". He has lost the kingdom, but the kingdom has not lost him. Notice that his worry is not selfish in the crude sense; it is about lineage, duty and whether the people are being governed by dharma. That is what makes it so hard to let go of. The Devi Mahatmya is unusually honest that the most binding attachments are the respectable ones, the ones that look like responsibility. The word "mine" is doing all the dragging.

## Meaning (hi)

यह राजा के मन की बात है, और पाठ उनके बोलने से पहले ही निदान कर देता है: उनका मन "ममत्व" — "मेरा" के भाव — से आकृष्ट, यानी घसीटा हुआ है। राज्य उनके हाथ से गया है, पर राज्य ने उन्हें नहीं छोड़ा। ग़ौर कीजिए कि उनकी चिन्ता मोटे अर्थ में स्वार्थी नहीं है; वह वंश, कर्तव्य और इस बात की है कि प्रजा का शासन धर्म से हो रहा है या नहीं। इसीलिए उसे छोड़ना इतना कठिन है। देवी माहात्म्य इस बात में असाधारण रूप से ईमानदार है कि सबसे कसकर बाँधने वाली आसक्तियाँ वे हैं जो सम्मानजनक दिखती हैं — जो ज़िम्मेदारी का रूप लेकर आती हैं। सारा खिंचाव "मेरा" शब्द में है।

## Reflection

Which of your worries looks like responsibility but is really the word "mine" refusing to let go?
