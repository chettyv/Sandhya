## Shloka

**Devanagari:** आत्मौपम्येन सर्वत्र समं पश्यति योऽर्जुन । / सुखं वा यदि वा दुःखं स योगी परमो मतः ॥6.32॥
**IAST:** ātmaupamyena sarvatra samaṃ paśyati yo'rjuna । / sukhaṃ vā yadi vā duḥkhaṃ sa yogī paramo mataḥ ॥6.32॥
**Say it:** aat-MOW-pum-YAY-na SAR-va-tra SA-mum PUSH-ya-ti YOR-joo-na / SOO-khum vaa YA-di vaa DOOH-khum sa YO-gee PA-ra-mo ma-taha
**Meaning:** One who sees the same everywhere by comparison with himself, Arjuna — whether in joy or in sorrow — that yogi is held to be the highest.

## Word by word

- **ātma-aupamyena** — by comparison with oneself
- **sarvatra** — everywhere
- **samam** — the same, equally
- **paśyati** — sees
- **yaḥ** — who
- **arjuna** — O Arjuna
- **sukham vā** — whether joy
- **yadi vā duḥkham** — or sorrow
- **saḥ yogī** — that yogi
- **paramaḥ** — the highest
- **mataḥ** — is held to be

## Meaning

A definition of the highest yogi that has nothing to do with feats of meditation: it is the one who measures everyone by their own self — who feels another's joy and pain as they feel their own. The Sanskrit ātmaupamyena, "by comparison with oneself," makes empathy a spiritual instrument, not a soft skill. The verse guards the whole chapter against a cold reading: mastery of the mind that does not widen into fellow-feeling has missed the point. For daily practice it is a portable test — before responding to anyone today, ask how the situation would land if it were yours. The person who lives that way, the verse says, is parama: supreme.

## Reflection

Whose joy or pain today can you measure by asking how it would feel as your own?
