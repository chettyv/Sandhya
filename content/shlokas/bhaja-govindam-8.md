**Say it:** kaa tay KAAN-taa KAS-tay PUT-rah / sam-SAA-ro-yam a-TEE-va vi-CHIT-rah / KAS-ya tvum kah KU-ta aa-YAA-tas / TAT-tvum CHIN-ta-ya TA-di-ha BHRAA-tah
**Meaning:** Who is your wife? Who is your son? Exceedingly strange is this round of the world. Whose are you? Who are you? From where have you come? Brother, ponder that truth here and now.

## Word by word

- **kā** — who (feminine)
- **te** — your
- **kāntā** — wife, beloved
- **kaḥ te putraḥ** — who is your son
- **saṃsāraḥ ayam** — this saṃsāra, this worldly round
- **atīva vicitraḥ** — exceedingly strange
- **kasya tvam** — whose are you
- **kaḥ** — who (are you)
- **kutaḥ āyātaḥ** — from where have you come
- **tattvam** — the truth, the reality
- **cintaya** — ponder
- **tat** — that
- **iha** — here, now
- **bhrātaḥ** — O brother

## Meaning

The questions sound cold until you notice the address: bhrātaḥ, brother — the speaker includes himself in the puzzle. Nothing here dismisses love or family; the verse asks what stands under the labels. Before this birth these particular bonds did not exist, and the "you" that owns them cannot itself be owned by them. Advaita hears the questions pointing past every role to the one Self that was never born into any of them. Devotional traditions ask the identical questions and answer differently — we belong, finally, to the Lord. Both agree the questions deserve asking now, while the roles are still warm, not at the end.

## Meaning (hi)

प्रश्न कठोर लगते हैं, जब तक सम्बोधन पर ध्यान न जाए: "भ्रातः" — भाई — कहकर वक्ता स्वयं को भी इसी पहेली में शामिल करता है। यहाँ प्रेम या परिवार का तिरस्कार नहीं है; श्लोक पूछता है कि इन नामों के नीचे खड़ा क्या है। इस जन्म से पहले ये सम्बन्ध नहीं थे, और जो "तुम" इन्हें अपना कहता है, वह स्वयं इनके स्वामित्व में नहीं आ सकता। अद्वैत इन प्रश्नों को हर भूमिका के पार उस एक आत्मा की ओर संकेत मानता है, जो किसी भूमिका में जन्मी ही नहीं। भक्ति-परम्पराएँ यही प्रश्न पूछकर उत्तर अलग देती हैं — अन्ततः हम भगवान के हैं। दोनों सहमत हैं: ये प्रश्न अभी पूछे जाने चाहिए, जब भूमिकाएँ जीवित हैं — अन्त में नहीं।

## Reflection

Before any of your roles existed — spouse, parent, child, professional — who were you?
